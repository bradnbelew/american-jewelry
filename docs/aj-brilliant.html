AJ in the brilliant profile

Your reference, as a mark. Table, girdle and pavilion at a 1.65 to 1 ratio, the proportions of a real round brilliant seen from the side. The letters are not placed inside the stone; they are built out of its facets. The A's two legs are crown facets that bend at the girdle and run on down to the culet, the way facet edges actually do. The J is the only curve in the drawing: light moving through the pavilion.

1

Profile

One line weight, eleven lines. Outline, girdle, the A and its crossbar, one bezel facet on the right, two pavilion facets, the J. Everything that is not a letter is a facet, and every letter is also a facet.

2

Brilliant

Your sketch's own language: thin facet lines, and two facets filled where the light catches. The two dark facets are the A and the J. The letters are read first; the stone is read a half second later.

3

Cut

The same stone, solid, with the A and J left open. The strongest of the three at a distance and on a dark box; the version for foil, enamel and the storefront.

With the name

The wide mark takes a wide wordmark. Placeholder serif, letterspaced; the real wordmark gets drawn once the mark is chosen.

Where I land

Profile is the mark. It is the one that is honest about what it is: a diamond drawn the way a jeweler draws one, where the lines happen to spell the house. Cut is the same drawing for places that need weight. Brilliant is the in-between and probably not needed. If this is the direction, the next pass is proportion work on the A (how wide it sits in the crown) and the J (how far the hook reaches), then the wordmark, then the stamp version, which will be the Cut silhouette at 2 mm with no interior lines at all.
