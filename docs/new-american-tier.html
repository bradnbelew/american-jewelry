The job of this tier

Every great house has a door people can walk through.

Ralph Lauren sells Purple Label to a few people and Polo to everyone, and Polo is what makes Purple Label possible. Armani has Emporio. The lower line pays the bills, builds the customer list, and introduces people to the house. It works only when it has its own name, its own look, and a clear place below the top.

New American has three jobs:

  1. Win the customer who would otherwise buy a lab diamond online. More than half of US engagement rings are now lab grown in recent surveys (The Knot, 2024). If we don't sell a man made diamond, that couple buys one somewhere else.
  2. Volume for the bench. Calibrated stones and fixed sizes keep the casting and setting schedule full and predictable, which pays for the one-of-one work.
  3. Start the relationship. Someone who buys a New American tennis bracelet at 27 is the person who buys Americana at 35 and commissions Royal American at 50. The line is built to move people up.
The hard truth, and the strategy it forces

The stone is a commodity. Sell the making.

Man made diamond prices have fallen steeply year after year and are still falling. Pandora moved its whole line to lab grown in 2021, and De Beers announced in 2025 that it was winding down its own lab-grown brand, Lightbox. A man made diamond has almost no resale value. Any brand that sells this tier as "a big diamond for less" is selling something that loses value the moment it leaves the store, and its prices look worse every year.

So New American is never priced or marketed on the stone. It is priced on the design, the solid gold, and the hands that made it. The man made diamond is the material that lets us do what natural stones make impossible at this price: full eternity bands, real tennis lines, 2 carat studs, all in top color and clarity.

Rule of the house: we never show price per carat on New American, never compare its price to a natural stone, and never discount it. The price belongs to the piece.

The name

Why "New American"

Put next to the other two tiers, it completes a sentence: Royal American is the past we're reaching for, Americana is the classic, and New American is the future. "New" says what the stones are without the word "lab," and it frames them as progress, not as a substitute. It speaks to a younger buyer, and it doesn't apologize.

Alternates if the trademark search or the family says no:

Foundry

We cast in house, so it's literally true. Industrial American heritage, in the spirit of Shinola and RRL. Harder edge, more masculine.

Filament

A thread of light and a thread of gold. Very ownable, fits delicate and tennis pieces well, weaker for bold statement pieces.

"New American" is a common phrase, so the protectable mark is the combination with the AJ monogram and the tier hallmark. A trademark screen is needed before anything is printed.

The campaign line

Edison didn't find light. He made it.

The American story is a story of making things. That is the honest, proud frame for a man made diamond, and nobody in the category is using it. The common lab-grown slogan, "made, not mined," is everywhere and sounds defensive. This line is offensive in the right sense.

The quality promise, published on every product page

The New American Standard

Color
D, E or F only. Colorless, every stone, including the small ones.
Clarity
VVS1 or VVS2. Nothing visible under a loupe at a glance.
Cut
Excellent or Ideal, with Hearts and Arrows on round centers.
Grading
Independent report (IGI) on every center stone 0.30 ct and above, laser inscribed.
Disclosure
Every listing says "man made diamond," and names the growth method and any post-growth treatment.
Metal
Solid 14k or 18k gold or platinum. Never plated, never vermeil, never hollow.
Made
Designed by our designer, cast and set at our bench in Tennessee.
Care
Lifetime cleaning, prong checks, and rhodium refresh at either store.

The point: our lowest tier carries a higher spec than most stores' best case. That's what makes it ours.

Design direction

Same house, different temperament

Bolder and cleaner

Americana is warm, engraved, storied. New American is crisp: high polish, sharp geometry, more stones, more light. Think architecture and machined precision, not heirloom.

Its own motifs

Designs are never shared with Americana outside bridal settings. New American gets motifs of its own, for example a "Grid" and a "Signal" line.

House signatures stay

The AJ hallmark, the small star inside every shank, the proportions and the finishing. You can tell it's ours. You can't mistake it for Americana.

Fewer, deeper designs

Three or four motifs at launch, each worked across the five families. Your brother draws the motif once; parametric CAD generates the sizes.

How it sits in the store

Concept layout, not to scale. New American sits at the front because it draws traffic and invites touching. The privacy and the price rise as you walk in.

Who buys it

Four customers to design for

The self-purchaser, 26–38

Buying her own first real piece: studs, a tennis bracelet, a stack band. Researches on her phone, buys in person if the store feels special.

The engaged couple

Wants a large, clean center stone and a custom feel on a set budget. Comes in for the ring builder and the fitting appointment.

The Nashville celebration

Nashville is one of the country's biggest bachelorette destinations. Bridesmaid gifts, bride gifts, and "we were here" pieces. A real local channel almost nobody is serving.

The men's buyer

Signets, tennis chains, cuban links. Music industry, athletes, Brentwood money. Buys bold and buys again.

How we talk about it
We say
  • Man made diamond (always, on every listing and tag)
  • Made in our Tennessee workshop
  • D color, VVS clarity, Ideal cut
  • The New American Standard
  • Made to order, ready in about two weeks
We never say
  • Cheaper, affordable alternative, budget
  • Fake, faux, simulant
  • "Diamond" with the qualifier dropped
  • Eco-friendly, sustainable, conflict-free (unless proven, item by item)
  • Investment, holds its value

The FTC Jewelry Guides require lab-grown stones to carry a clear qualifier every time. "Man made" should qualify as a phrase of the same meaning, but have a lawyer confirm it before launch. Environmental claims fall under the FTC Green Guides and need proof.

The upgrade promise

A ladder, not a dead end

Because a man made diamond holds little resale value, the customer's fear is "I'll want a natural stone later." Answer it: within a set number of years, a New American bridal or solitaire purchase earns a fixed credit toward an Americana piece. That turns the tier into the first step of a lifelong relationship, and it moves people up the ladder on purpose.

The credit amount and window need to be modeled against margins before it's offered. It's a strong idea only if the numbers hold.

One question changes everything

Do we grow our own diamonds?

You said "we design, grow, and cast." If "grow" means we grow man made diamonds ourselves, this tier has the strongest story in the company. Natural diamonds can't be mined in America at commercial scale, so Americana can never be fully American-made. A man made diamond grown in Tennessee, set in recycled gold, at our bench, can. The lowest tier would carry the purest "made in America" claim in the house, and almost no jeweler in the country can say it.

If "grow" means printing wax or resin models for casting, the concept above still stands. We'd source US-grown stones where possible and say "designed and handcrafted in Tennessee."

Guardrails

What would sink it

Sharing designs downward

The moment an Americana design shows up with man made stones at a third of the price, Americana loses its value.

Competing on price

Online lab brands will always be cheaper. We win on design, the spec, and the store, and we stop there.

Letting it take over the store

It will likely sell the most units. It should still take the smallest share of the brand's attention in ads and window displays.

Sloppy disclosure

One listing that drops "man made" is a legal and reputational problem. Build the disclosure into the product templates so it can't be left off.

Decisions for the family
  1. The name: New American, Foundry, Filament, or something else.
  2. Whether we grow our own stones, and if so, at what volume.
  3. Which three or four motifs your brother starts with for this tier.
  4. The price bands, once cost of goods per family is known.
  5. Whether to offer the upgrade promise, and on what terms.
  6. Box and hallmark colors, after the monogram is chosen.
