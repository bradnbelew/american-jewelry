# American Jewelry: an independent creative review

**Astra · 13 October 2026 · Private mockup review**

**Verdict:** American Jewelry has built a disciplined luxury presentation without yet making a persuasive designer brand. It has more rules about how a house behaves than visible evidence of who designs the jewelry, why the designs matter, or what it feels like to belong here. The opportunity is substantial: one designer, a working bench, two real Tennessee stores, and customers who already trust the family. Those are stronger raw materials than cream paper, velvet, registry numerals, or the word “house.”

I would keep the ambition and change the center of gravity. Make the designer's judgment and the family's continuing care visible. Give the jewelry more room to be compared and worn. Stop treating ordinary shopping clarity as a threat to luxury.

## Scope and evidence

I inspected the [live Hydrogen storefront][live] on desktop and at **390 × 844**, including every requested route: home, all eight menu panels, Jewelry, Rings and filters, Royal American, Americana, New American, Signal Solitaire and its light viewer, Designs, Collections, Search, Bag, Stores, and House. I also inspected the ring builder on phone. Desktop review included a 1440 × 1000 viewport. These were browser viewport checks, not tests on physical phones.

Interaction checks included desktop filtering, phone filter expansion, search queries, product configuration, the light viewer's close-up, and adding a Signal Solitaire to the bag with size 6.5. The bag settled at $3,200 with the chosen configuration. I removed that test item afterward. I did not place an order, submit an appointment, or test payment completion. I did not assess the temporary photographs' quality or the temporary 3D model's fidelity.

The source review uses repository commit **`5f71551b0ef5224acaf7b077e9effb46f6c7e573`**, with links pinned to that revision. The live deployment was inspected separately and was not proven to match that commit. I read the canon, art direction, decisions, open questions, research index and research/17, relevant council verdicts, navigation and relevant route/component code. The requested `mockups/viewing/` directory was absent from this checkout. I read the recorded rejection and subsequent “two lights” decision; I am not claiming to have viewed that prototype.

The external comparisons use ten official sites: all five named luxury houses, two modern direct brands, and three outside jewelry. They are focused page/content comparisons, not complete usability audits of those businesses. Cartier's reference was available through indexed official content; the other references were read from official pages. Judgments about memorability and purchase friction are professional assessments, not measured conversion results or customer research.

## 1. The first 60 seconds

**First ten seconds:** a serious regional jeweler has hired a restrained editorial designer. The centered, widely spaced wordmark, two-row header, hand on linen, large serif headline and subdued paper ground communicate expense and good manners. They also belong to a familiar contemporary luxury vocabulary. “An American house of fine jewelry” describes the desired category. It does not give me a reason to choose this particular business. [Home][live]

**Ten to thirty seconds:** I encounter Royal American, Americana and New American before I have learned a recognizable design. The site asks me to absorb its organization early. The tiny Tennessee place line is more specific than the enormous headline. That hierarchy is backward.

**Thirty to sixty seconds:** Shooting Star on green leather gives me an object and a motif I can remember. The numbered Tennessee registry introduces another promising idea. But the family's actual people and a consequential design decision still have not appeared. The home then offers an index with **10 rings, 6 necklaces, 1 earring style, and 4 bracelets**. That is candid, but it punctures the intended impression of breadth without adding much shopping help.

Would I remember it tomorrow? **The star and perhaps “The Cumberland.” Probably not the typography, the headline, or the family.** That is my own recall judgment, not a finding from customers. The current home gives a stranger very little human material to retain.

The danger is not that the site looks inexpensive. It is that it looks expensive in a way that can be rented. The parts that cannot be rented—the brother's design judgment, an actual repair, a customer returning, a specific choice at the bench—are mostly described at a distance.

## 2. Hide the name

Covering the wordmark would leave these screens largely interchangeable:

| Exact screen | Why it fails | What would make it identifiable |
|---|---|---|
| Home, first viewport | Hand, linen, serif and a category declaration. Nothing in the composition depends on this designer. | One recognizable motif plus a real decision from its drawing, with authorship attached. |
| Americana, opening image and title | Green leather creates atmosphere; a named leather color is not a brand asset by itself. | Show what Shooting Star does across a ring, pendant and bracelet, with its distinctive geometry legible. |
| New American, opening and standard table | Pale stone, solitaire and grading terminology could introduce many man-made diamond sellers. | Let Signal's setting or Grid's repetition lead; put the standard beside the buying decision. |
| Signal Solitaire, desktop buy area | Familiar gallery, title, price, metal choices, size, accordions. This useful commerce structure is not the problem; the absence of specific authorship is. | Explain one reason for the head, shoulder or band using this actual piece. |
| Designs, introductory paragraph | “Motif,” “line,” and rules about designs crossing lines sound like a catalog schema. | A visual index of four unmistakable shapes, followed by their stories. |
| Collections, entire index | Four ruled text rows with counts; “Family” and “The Stack” promise human occasions but show none. | One composed wearing example per real use, linked to the actual constituent pieces. |
| House, opening workshop image and definition | Anonymous hands plus a definition of a house is generic craft rhetoric, even after a real shoot replaces the stand-in. | A named person making a specific decision, followed by a piece that resulted from it. |
| Bag, navy ground and packaging | Navy and a box can feel premium but are transferable to almost any jeweler. | Exact configuration, expected next step, and a small glimpse of this family's actual packing/care practice. |

**The registry has the strongest existing claim to identity.** Its numbering, Tennessee names and continuing record can accumulate meaning. But numbers do not manufacture provenance. The open questions explicitly say the three Royal pieces and their specifications may be placeholders. Until that is settled, this is a promising publishing structure rather than proof of a collecting house. [Royal American][royal] · [Open questions A13][questions]

The green Shooting Star plate is the strongest existing image *placement*: large enough to make the motif matter. Preserve that confidence in scale. Do not conclude that green leather itself is now an owned visual asset.

## 3. Ten reference sites: what to take, what to resist

The useful comparison is a behavior or a piece of evidence, not “make it look like Cartier.”

Do not borrow Cartier's age or use a history-shaped layout without history. |
| **[Tiffany — The Landmark](https://www.tiffany.com/stores/en/united-states/new-york-the-landmark-fifth-avenue-1)** | Makes a location both a destination and a useful place: directions, hours, appointments and specific services coexist. | AJ's stores start with a broad statement and a large image before the practical details. **Take the combination of invitation and logistics:** make it easy to decide which store to visit and what can happen there. A flagship fantasy is unnecessary. |

| **[VRAI — Custom engagement rings](https://www.vrai.com/custom-engagement-rings)** | Explains the design process and the progression through consultation, visualization and approval. | AJ's builder gives choices, a code and “Request this ring,” but the commitment can feel unclear. **Take explicit expectations:** what the request sends, who responds, what is estimated, and when a purchase becomes an order. |

Optional bench sound can support making; it should not become a compulsory soundtrack. |

Existing customers can provide depth that a new house collection cannot honestly claim yet. |

These references also expose a false choice in the current direction: serious houses can tell rich stories, show multiple objects, use functional controls and make services visible. None of those acts automatically makes a site ordinary.

## 4. Section-by-section changes

### Brand, naming and voice

**Keep American Jewelry for this exercise.** The supplied business has existing recognition and about 2,500 reviews; a new name would discard something real before a replacement has earned anything. But it is a broad category name. Typography alone will not make it distinctive. Pair it consistently with one recognizable design and identifiable people. The exact review count in the canon is dated August 26, 2026; it is not independently verified here as today's count. [Canon §1][canon]

**Royal American is the weakest line name.** “Royal” borrows a status vocabulary while the desired distinction is an American family with its own workshop. It can survive if the objects earn the grandeur, but currently the registry concept is more interesting than the title. Test **The Registry** as the navigation label, with Royal American retained as the line designation. This is a naming recommendation, not a claim of trademark availability.

**Americana and New American are too similar to do explanatory work unaided.** One says a cultural tradition; the other suggests contemporary style or new arrivals. Neither reliably communicates stone origin. Keep the names provisionally, but attach short material descriptors where people choose between them. Also correct the home shorthand “Americana / Natural diamonds”: that line includes solid gold and birthstones too. [Home][live] · [Americana][americana]

Shooting Star and Prism are more useful than the line names because they describe forms. Signal is serviceable. Grid needs a strong visual demonstration or it stays a generic construction term. Promote the motifs; subordinate the administrative hierarchy.

Retire “So we stopped being a store.” You still have two stores, and being useful to the community is part of the evidence for trust. The family should explain its new authorship without disowning the reason people already come in. Likewise, “we would like to be” is hesitant positioning. The better opening is a demonstrable statement about who draws, sets, repairs and stands behind a piece. [House][house]

### Menu and tabs

The new panels have useful depth. They are also **eight department signs for 21 pieces**, plus search, appointment, saved items, bag and account. Three neighboring line tabs require a first-time visitor to learn the brand's structure before choosing a ring. The Jewelry panel then offers kinds, designs and collections; some destinations recur under several headings. That multiplication creates the appearance of breadth but quickly reveals the same inventory. [Navigation source][nav-code]

I would test six primary labels:

**Jewelry · Bridal · The Registry · Design & making · Services · Visit**

Keep Americana and New American visible inside Jewelry with material descriptions. Put the family, four designs, drawings and House letter inside Design & making. Keep search conspicuous; move account into a lower-priority utility position. Use **Saved pieces** in the navigation while retaining **Keep** as the action if desired. “Kept” is elegant internal vocabulary with an unnecessary interpretation cost.

Panel-specific changes:

- **Jewelry:** lead with four kind links and four visual motif samples. Keep one clear “All jewelry,” not “Every piece” plus “All of Jewelry.” A visual index is navigation, not a promotion.
- **Bridal:** distinguish “Choose a finished design” from “Build a ring.” Put one clear builder entry beside the settings, rather than making three near-identical builder routes feel like separate ranges. Keep sizing and natural/man-made guidance.
- **Royal American:** retain the three named registry entries. Put each thumbnail beside its number; the current single mood image cannot help compare the three objects.
- **Americana / New American, if retained as tabs:** make the difference understandable in the first line. Shorten repeated descriptions and show the motifs before a directory of piece types.
- **Services:** lead with verbs people arrive needing: repair, resize, engrave, reset. State how to begin and where to bring the piece after location facts are verified.
- **Stores:** make the two actual locations immediately actionable, with directions and verified hours. “All of Stores” is awkward system-generated language for two places.
- **The House:** the current panel gives substantial space to policies and technical journal titles. Lead with designer and family; move routine small print to the footer.

The desktop trigger also needs one predictable action. Source code opens panels on hover/focus, while clicking an already-open trigger navigates to its page; I encountered a click taking me to a line page during panel exploration. Separate a panel button from its “View all” link. Keyboard panel access and Escape worked in my later checks, so this is a consistency recommendation, not a claim that keyboard navigation is wholly broken. [Header source][header-code]

On phone, the eight accordions reproduce the depth faithfully, but turn the menu into a long textual directory. Preserve useful destinations while reducing repetition. Put the most common shopping paths and search first.

### Search, shopping selector and filters

The live search tests were revealing:

| Query | Observed result | Change |
|---|---|---|
| `Signal` | Five answers: a design, builder and three products. | Good breadth. Separate product results from reading/help and give products clear visual priority. |
| `lab grown` | One answer: the Natural or man-made explanatory page; no products. | Recognize common customer wording and return qualifying products while keeping “man-made diamond” in their displayed names. |
| `singal` | Zero answers and “Nothing in the house answers to that.” | Offer Signal as a correction. Replace the aloof sentence with a helpful next step. |

The empty [search page][search] is an input above a large footer. Offer four designs, product kinds and practical suggestions such as ring sizing and repairs. These should be editorial shortcuts, not fabricated trending searches.

The [Jewelry index][jewelry] puts the three price-on-request Royal pieces first. That makes a general shopping entrance feel like an appointment-only collection. Keep a distinct registry entrance, then show a considered mixture of purchasable forms and prices. Do not label an unverified editorial selection “Bestsellers.”

The category links are useful; the staggered grid is less useful when comparing. On desktop the middle column is offset; on phone the even entries are offset. This makes a small selection take more vertical space without giving it more substance. Use aligned products for shopping and reserve asymmetry for editorial features. [Catalog component][catalog-component]

The filter model also mixes unrelated things: **Solid gold** and **One of one** appear under Stone, alongside natural and man-made diamonds. More seriously, the source classifies a one-of-one piece only as one of one before checking stones. The Cumberland is described in the live registry as a natural diamond, yet the Natural diamond filter excludes that class of product. A shopper should not need to understand your merchandising hierarchy to find its stones. Separate material, stone origin and production status. [Rings][rings] · [Filter source, `stonesOf`][browse-code]

Price filtering uses a product's overall starting price independently of selected stone/metal. That is a **source-level risk**, not an end-to-end failure I reproduced with a purchase: combined filters may admit a product whose matching configuration costs more than the selected budget. Filter and label the matching configuration, or clearly state the limitation. [Filter source, `filterPieces`][browse-code]

At phone width the expanded filter region was roughly **1,527 CSS pixels tall**. It pushes the products well down the page. Desktop selections updated automatically; phone selections wait for Apply. Use a concise filter sheet with collapsible groups, a visible result count and a persistent “Show pieces” action. Selected filters need visible removal controls, whatever the canon thinks about chips.

The builder has more potential than another filter group. Its design code, exact configuration, price explanation and save/share controls make a small range feel configurable. But it initially sends a customer into a choices-and-request process. Explain the next step beside **Request this ring**, preserve the selected configuration, and offer human help without requiring completion of the form. The phone opener already gives the object and selected price reasonable prominence. Do not bury that strength beneath the repeated essays about all three settings. [Ring builder][builder]

### Heroes and the home sequence

The current opener recipe—full-bleed image, lower-left serif title, overlay—has become its own template across home, lines and House. Changing the photo ground does not change the underlying idea.

Give each entrance a different job:

- **Home:** prove authorship with one specific design decision.
- **Americana:** show a family of forms worn together, with a direct path to its pieces.
- **New American:** demonstrate a precise design, then state the stone standard.
- **Royal American:** introduce one verified object as a dossier, with an immediately reachable registry.
- **Stores:** help someone visit; practical information should not wait beneath a cinematic opener.
- **House:** introduce people and evidence, rather than define “house.”

My proposed home order is: **one authored object → four designs seen clearly → a real maker/care story → selected pieces to shop → one registry object → two stores.** The opening can have a primary “Explore Shooting Star” and a secondary “All jewelry.” Two relevant routes are not a failure of confidence.

Move the text-only line classification out of the second screen. Remove public category counts from the home, particularly “Earrings 1.” Keep honest counts in actual search and filtered results. Replace the defensive “We are not the first…” block with an actual event or decision from the business. Changing its font size will not turn it into a reason to buy.

### Line pages, Designs and Collections

**Royal American:** this has the most coherent atmosphere. Keep the darkness as a deliberate episode. But the object needs a story before it needs another declaration of rarity. For each verified piece, show a large image, exact stone information, the designer's reason for the setting, a view of construction, and current availability. Clarify “In workshop, spring” with a real date or a confirmed status; an unqualified season is ambiguous in October. On phone, the registry hides its Stones column while retaining number, name and state. Replace that table with stacked entries that preserve the stone information. [Royal][royal] · [Line route][line-code]

**Americana:** the opener suggests tactile richness; the product ledger immediately turns the jewelry into small thumbnails, long metal lists and prices. The source allocates images only 80 pixels wide on phone and 120 on desktop in these rows. That is an inventory view. Give Shooting Star and Prism each a substantial lead piece, a worn composition and a compact aligned family of siblings. Reduce repetitive metal prose to a concise option summary. Let visitors see enough form to decide which object to open. [Americana][americana] · [Piece ledger][catalog-component]

**New American:** the standard is a useful promise in the wrong position. The full grading table arrives before the product families, particularly conspicuous on phone. Lead with a compelling Signal or Grid composition, show pieces, and keep a short standard beside the selection with full detail available immediately below. Do not reduce this line to a technical alternative to natural diamonds. The same investment in design and wearing experience should be visible here. [New American][new-american]

**Designs:** promote this from a definitions page to the visual center of the house. Show the four motifs together first. Then give each an annotated origin, useful dimensions, a worn view and every actual piece carrying it. Delete the absolute “Designs never cross from one line to another”: Signal's product copy and the bridal builder already explain shared settings. The canon includes that exception; the public paragraph does not. [Designs][designs] · [Signal][signal] · [Canon §3][canon]

**Collections:** Family, Bridal, Signets and The Stack are lenses over existing pieces, not additional inventory. Give each lens a reason to exist: a real birthstone arrangement, a compatible band/engagement pairing, actual engraving examples, and stacks whose fit has been checked. The current text-only index exposes tiny counts without illustrating the promised life. Do not add more occasion pages until they contain a different selection or useful advice. [Collections][collections]

### Product page and “See it in the light”

The desktop Signal page has the essentials: visible price, qualified stone description, lead time, metal and carat choices, engraving, store appointment and detailed specifications. The bag carried my selected size correctly. Keep that functional foundation.

Its story is weak: the first sentence spends scarce attention explaining the catalog's cross-line exception. Begin with why this setting exists and what the wearer experiences. Put the natural/man-made alternative beside the relevant choice.

The phone ordering is a more urgent issue. **The gallery runs before the product title and form**, while a sticky $3,200/Add to bag bar already appears over the early photographs. The initial size is **7**. The source confirms this is a preset, not a deliberate choice from the visitor. A purchase action should not quietly endorse a default fit before the customer reaches the options. [Signal][signal] · [PieceForm initial size][form-code]

Reorder phone content: first image → title, price, stone and lead time → required choices → further gallery and details. A compact gallery with explicit thumbnails or swipe plus controls is appropriate. Before a size is selected, the sticky action should be **Choose size**, taking the shopper to that control. Show the chosen size and metal when it becomes Add to bag.

Keep stone origin, selected configuration, lead time and key return restrictions close to purchase. Long technical detail can fold; purchase-critical information should not depend on opening four accordions. Show an actual report when a stone has been assigned, and explain the assignment process when it has not. Do not imply a generic grading standard is an individual stone report.

The light viewer opened on both desktop and phone, with an object view, **Look closer**, stone information and **Step back**. Its concentrated darkness gives the object attention. I would keep this optional entry and reject a site-wide dark-room treatment. But a rotatable sparkling ring is a capability, not yet a brand idea. Give the light an authored purpose: compare controlled daylight, evening light and a neutral inspection view of the same selected piece. Label simulations honestly, retain a still fallback and make returning to the exact configuration effortless. Physical-device tilt behavior was not tested.

“Complete the set” is also too automatic for a solitaire, studs and a men's signet. **More in Signal** is accurate. Reserve set language for combinations that have been designed and photographed together.

### Bag, stores and House

**Bag:** the navy treatment is a useful change of atmosphere. The configuration information is valuable. The packaging block is in the wrong place: in my phone bag view, it consumed the early screen while subtotal and checkout sat below. Reorder to item and configuration → subtotal and checkout → delivery/return essentials → compact packaging preview. The buyer has already chosen; help them finish. A large box image is not worth making the total harder to find. [Bag][bag]

**Stores:** surface address, hours, directions, telephone and the reason to choose a location before the large image. Replace “Book an hour” with task-specific choices and state whether the customer is requesting a time or securing a confirmed booking. Show who will meet them, once names and consent are available. Tiffany's location page demonstrates that utility can sit inside a luxury presentation.

There is also a factual inconsistency to resolve. The live Stores and House pages assign making/casting and a glass-fronted bench to Mt. Juliet. The open questions still mark bench location/form and in-house casting unresolved and instruct the briefs to say “our workshop.” The store hours are explicitly provisional on the page. These are acceptable items to leave visibly unresolved in a private mockup; they cannot become confident public copy by repetition. I am identifying a source conflict, not asserting where the bench actually is. [Stores][stores] · [House][house] · [Open questions A1, A4–5 and C1][questions]

**House:** replace the abstract definition and unsigned institutional letter with a family introduction, the designer's actual name when approved, one consequential design story, and one piece of evidence of continuing care. “The family” is not a substitute for a signature when the proposition is personal authorship.

Delete **The type** from the customer-facing House page. It spends a substantial paragraph defending Source Serif and Public Sans while the designer remains unnamed. That is a design team's rationale occupying the space a customer needs for the maker. Keep typography decisions in the design brief.

### Mobile verdict

The site generally becomes legible single-column content; I did not observe horizontal overflow in the inspected catalog and line layouts. But “stack the desktop page” is not sufficient mobile art direction. It produces the long filter block, gallery-before-choice product page, tiny ledger thumbnails, hidden registry stone facts and packaging-before-total bag. Those are priority problems of sequence and information, not requests for another round of margin polishing.

## 5. Rules that are holding the site back

The bans on invented heritage, false scarcity, misleading stone descriptions and unsupported origin claims should remain. The following aesthetic rules have not earned the same authority.

| Existing rule | Why I disagree | Replacement rule |
|---|---|---|
| **No line drawings.** [Canon, Never do][canon] | The brother drawing every piece is the central authorship claim. Banning his most direct evidence while allowing generic craft language is backward. Research/17 already proposes the drawing beneath the piece, exposing an unresolved conflict inside the system. | Allow authentic design drawings, revisions and working annotations. Ban meaningless decorative pseudo-sketches, not drawings. |
| **One image per screen; a same-piece pair at most once per page.** [Art direction §3][art] | Jewelry is understood through scale, relationships and comparison. This rule helps explain why the collections are text and the line pages become ledgers. | Let the purpose determine quantity. Use one image for drama, several for comparison and actual worn combinations. |
| **One metal per screen.** [Art direction §2][art] | A customer choosing yellow gold or platinum benefits from seeing both. The rule protects a color composition at the expense of the decision. Cartier's Trinity is an obvious counterexample to treating mixed metal as inherently incoherent. | Keep metal rendering accurate and differences clear. Permit comparisons and intentional mixed-metal styling. |
| **Line color must live in photographs, never CSS.** [Canon palette][canon] | This keeps the same paper expanse dominant whenever the image ends, especially through long phone pages. The owner has explicitly objected to the cream-store effect. Replacing one off-white with another is too small a response. | Establish a restrained but real secondary palette for section grounds, selected states and editorial moments. Maintain contrast; do not assign every line a permanent costume. |
| **Say each sentence once per site; over 30 words becomes an accordion/table/journal.** [Canon voice][canon] | People can enter at a product or search result. They need the same essential fact there. The council spends attention on repeated home/builder language that may be useful in both places. Mechanical word limits also hide meaning before a story can develop. | Repeat essential identity and purchase facts at the point of need. Edit for usefulness and reading rhythm, not a site-wide sentence inventory. |
| **No equal tile rows, chips or carousels.** [Canon, Never do][canon] | Aligned comparison, removable filter selections and a compact mobile image gallery solve real tasks. Their presence does not dictate a discount-store aesthetic. The current staggered catalog and long phone gallery show the cost of a blanket ban. | Ban gratuitous interface decoration and autoplay promotional sliders. Permit familiar controls where they reduce effort. |
| **Reviews only as one dated store-page count; no product-page reviews.** [Canon, Never do][canon] | AJ has substantial existing service trust but asks strangers to accept a newly constructed house identity on assertion. A count alone cannot explain what customers value. | Use a small amount of permissioned, accurately attributed service evidence near care/visit decisions. Do not present a store-service review as a review of a new design. Product reviews must concern the actual product. |
| **No model faces except family.** [Canon, Never do][canon] | A family can be central without being the only people allowed a face. The current anonymity keeps jewelry detached from the lives it enters. | Photograph real wearers with consent, credit and clear context. Casting can be deliberate without becoming faceless. |
| **At most one action and constantly changing compositions.** [Design principles][design-file] | One decision can require two valid routes: buy online or see in store; compare materials or ask for help. Meanwhile, compulsory layout variation makes the shopping grid less regular without making the designs more distinct. | Give each screen one primary task, with useful subordinate choices. Repeat functional layouts; vary the editorial argument. |

**The test should be whether a rule makes the object, person or decision clearer.** Passing a screenshot rubric is insufficient. Keep the excellent legibility and factual discipline. Remove rules that mainly ensure the site resembles its own moodboard.

## 6. Three genuinely different directions

These are alternative creative systems, not three palettes applied to the existing page recipe. Color values below are starting points for design exploration, not a tested accessible token set. Typeface descriptions establish a direction without prescribing an unreviewed font purchase.

### A. The Working Jeweler — recommended

**One-line idea:** You can see the judgment that made the piece.

**Home:** a finished Shooting Star fills roughly two-thirds of the opening composition. Beside it sits a crop of the brother's actual drawing, including one revision that mattered. A short annotation explains the change in plain language. Below, all four motifs appear in a precise visual index, followed by the maker and a real bench story. The four motifs can be compared on one screen; they are not four successive hero banners.

**Product page:** the object remains large, but the page has the intelligence of a good workshop presentation: finished piece, worn scale, useful side view, one annotated detail and a compact purchase column. Specifications align neatly. A “Why this shape” section contains an actual answer, not a manifesto. Selecting metal or stone changes the appropriate views and facts; fit must be chosen explicitly.

**Type and color:** a sturdy grotesk in medium/bold weights for names and navigation, readable book typography only for longer stories, and restrained technical numerals where dimensions need them. Chalk white, graphite and a controlled cobalt accent—approximately `#FAFAF7`, `#202326`, `#2548B8`. Real drawing paper and workshop materials bring variation. This is visibly firmer and less courtly than the current all-regular serif system.

**Signature interaction:** **The decision.** A two-state control aligns one authentic earlier contour with the final piece, making a meaningful change understandable. Buttons work as well as dragging. A static two-frame version conveys the same point. No invented sketches, dates or supposed revisions.

**Why this family and Tennessee:** one brother supplies authorship; the bench can explain consequences; two stores let a visitor meet the people behind the work. It earns an American identity through observable behavior and actual addresses without flags or borrowed aristocracy.

**Risk:** it can become an engineering portfolio. Counter that with worn images, warmth and people. Do not publish tolerances and CAD screenshots merely because they exist. Each detail must explain beauty, comfort or longevity.

**What changes strategically:** “house” becomes the visitor's conclusion rather than the site's most repeated claim.

### B. In Good Company

**One-line idea:** Fine jewelry belongs in a life, and this family stays in that life.

**Home:** an editorial opening built around a real gathering or appointment, with faces and several pieces deliberately worn together. Two or three differently scaled images share a screen. A person's short, specific story leads into a shoppable selection. An adjacent invitation introduces the actual people at Brentwood and Mt. Juliet. The stores become places with hosts, not coordinates at the bottom.

**Product page:** begin with a piece in use and a compact factual buying area. Show how it sits, what pairs with it and how someone has made it their own. An engraving example can explain a real occasion with consent. A quiet service record can document subsequent care. Product details remain rigorous; sentiment never replaces materials or fit.

**Type and color:** a broad, confident slab serif for occasional headlines with a warm humanist sans for reading and controls. Bright white, oxblood and clear sky blue—approximately `#FFFFFF`, `#782D38`, `#9DC3D8`—with black body text. Color appears in editorial panels as well as images. The rhythm resembles a lively family publication rather than an auction catalog.

**Signature interaction:** **Set out three pieces.** Select up to three actual products into a simple comparison tray, see their proportions and prices together, then carry that exact selection into a store-visit request. It is a digital version of a thoughtful counter appointment. Do not promise store availability unless confirmed.

**Why this family and Tennessee:** the business already has relationships and two local places to continue them. A real returning customer and a familiar counter are more credible than pretending the collection has a century of history.

**Risk:** lifestyle imagery can overwhelm jewelry and become generic Southern nostalgia. Avoid staged “heritage” props, assumptions about family structure and stock celebration narratives. Let actual people and actual pieces provide specificity.

**What changes strategically:** the family's existing reputation becomes part of the luxury proposition, rather than something the new house identity must escape.

### C. Jewelry in Full Light

**One-line idea:** A few objects, shown with extraordinary visual conviction.

**Home:** a single real design occupies a bold, sharply colored field, with a moving reflection or shadow that explains its form. The next scene changes scale abruptly: a wearable view, then a close study of the design's geometry. Instead of an institutional line introduction, visitors move between four visual studies: star, facet, suspended stone and grid. Shopping remains one click away.

**Product page:** a large object stage sits above or beside a compact, high-contrast purchase area. A controlled light comparison keeps the piece, framing and scale consistent. Dimensions, stone qualification, price and delivery remain plainly visible on a neutral reading surface. Each visual demonstration answers something about the object; it does not make users wait through a performance.

**Type and color:** assertive condensed sans headlines, a straightforward sans for controls, and no default editorial serif. Ultramarine, pale mineral gray and a tightly controlled citron accent—approximately `#243DAE`, `#F0F2F3`, `#DCEB70`. Use dark episodes selectively; this does not restore the rejected all-dark site.

**Signature interaction:** **Change the light, keep the piece.** Switch among three documented lighting setups, with a reduced-motion still alternative. At one point the ring and its reflection make an unusually clear, shareable composition. Use actual footage where possible; simulations must not overpromise the stone's behavior.

**Why this family and Tennessee:** a small catalog permits exceptional attention per object. The designer and bench can explain exactly what each light reveals. The stores give people a place to check the experience in person; Tennessee remains real context, not a decorative regional theme.

**Risk:** a sophisticated demo can be memorable while the brand remains forgettable. It also costs more to produce accurately. Attach every visual study to a named design decision and a usable product route. Do not build it until representative assets and mobile performance are viable.

**What changes strategically:** design confidence replaces inherited luxury manners. This is the highest visual risk and the highest production burden of the three.

Choose **A** as the core direction. It depends on a differentiator the brief says already exists. B is a credible alternative if the family prefers social warmth; C is the alternative if the ambition is a contemporary design house with a strong visual production budget. Combining all three on the home would dissolve the choice.

## 8. Make 21 pieces feel deep without making them look numerous

**The ambition should be a large body of knowledge around a focused collection.** The present design spreads 21 pieces across many entrances, but too often changes the heading rather than the experience. “Bridal — 2 pieces” and “Earrings — 1” make that visible. Greater depth comes from distinct evidence and useful relationships.

| Structure | Publish only this | How it adds depth | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Four design dossiers** | Real drawings, intent, decisions, dimensions, worn views and current pieces for Shooting Star, Prism, Signal and Grid. | Each motif becomes a body of work with different things to learn and compare. | Do not fabricate an origin story to match a name. |
| **Royal registry** | Verified objects, assigned number, exact stone record, current status and date of status. Preserve a record after acquisition where appropriate. | A continuing catalog of work grows naturally beyond current availability. | Do not backfill fake sales, claim a placeholder is available, or expose owners without permission. |
| **Documented work archive** | Actual earlier commissions and completed pieces with real dates where known. Distinguish AJ design, restoration and third-party originals. | The business can have a history of work even if the current house collection is new. | Unknown dates stay unknown. A rendering under consideration is a study, not an archived sale. |
| **Variation atlas** | A concise visual matrix of genuinely offered metals, stones and sizes on a design page. | Shows the extent of choice without duplicating each variant as another product. | Respect actual combinations and configuration-specific price/lead time. Do not multiply options into a fake catalog count. |
| **Bench casebook** | One problem, the intervention, the outcome and the person responsible, with consent where needed. | Repair, setting, engraving and CAD reveal competence through cases. | Document precisely which operations AJ performed. |
| **Wear and fit library** | Actual ring heights, band pairings, necklace lengths, on-body scale and checked stacks. | Helps people imagine use and compares the same designs in meaningful ways. | A scale graphic is illustrative unless calibrated; never pretend every screen shows life size. |
| **Hosted selection** | A saved group of real pieces and an appointment request carrying the configuration/context. | Converts a small catalog into personal service without pretending every person needs a custom design. | Keep availability and appointment confirmation explicit. |
| **Care record** | Real service milestones and care guidance; offer a private customer record only if the business can maintain it. | Gives ownership continuing substance. | Promise the record only when the operating process exists. Public stories require permission. |

Prioritize four design dossiers, three verified registry entries if those objects exist, and a small set of strong bench cases. Those are editorial units built from real work, not extra products. Add material when it earns a different page. A thin journal full of generic buying advice would merely produce another form of padding.

Useful relationships should be visible: **design → forms → legitimate variations → worn combinations → making → care**. A visitor can explore much more than 21 purchase records without being misled about how much inventory exists.

## 9. The team's five largest likely mistakes

1. **Confusing compliance with distinction.** The home council verdict awards a distinctiveness pass for Source Serif on paper, navy velvet, the provenance line and a hand frame. It simultaneously acknowledges that the opening could belong to another contemporary jeweler. These are ingredients or facts; the review has not demonstrated audience recognition. Add an external recall/comparison test before declaring ownership. [Home verdict, D2–D4][home-verdict]

2. **Assuming that removing retail conventions creates a house.** The bans on equal rows, chips and carousels help produce staggered shopping, a very long phone filter and a gallery that precedes the purchasing facts. Good commerce needs predictable structure. The authored content should supply distinction; the buyer should not have to relearn comparison. [Canon][canon] · [Catalog component][catalog-component] · [Signal][signal]

3. **Treating institutional modesty as an emotionally strong proposition.** The council's single proposed home improvement is enlarging “We are not the first American jewelry house.” That introduces an unnamed superior before offering a reason to care about AJ. The House page compounds it with “we would like to be” and a long type explanation. Replace the whole argument with demonstrated authorship, not a larger negative sentence. [Home verdict, final recommendation][home-verdict] · [House][house]

4. **Trying to look established by building more taxonomy.** Three lines, four designs, four collections, four product kinds and eight top-level tabs are different views of 21 products. The Collections page's small counts expose that immediately. The actual source of scale should be work, variation, making and relationships. [Navigation][nav-code] · [Collections][collections] · [Open questions B33][questions]

5. **Allowing elegant copy to outrun verified facts.** The published mockup speaks confidently about casting here, a glass-fronted Mt. Juliet bench and named Royal pieces, while the source register leaves those facts unresolved. The system is exceptionally strict about tiny typographic departures and much less consistent about this boundary. Resolve the factual register before turning the mockup into a public promise. This criticism concerns the contradiction, not the truth of any unverified claim. [House][house] · [Stores][stores] · [Open questions A4–5, A12–13, C1][questions]
