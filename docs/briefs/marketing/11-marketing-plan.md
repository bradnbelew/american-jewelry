## The frame

A brick-and-mortar luxury business, not ecommerce-first. The site wins the Consider phase and hands off to an appointment or a store visit. The biggest leak today is local search and location pages. Promotions are releases and access, never price.

## Channels and their jobs

|---|---|---|

| Instagram | Educational, never salesy | The Drawing (30 s, the designer's hand, weekly); From the Bench (15-60 s, twice weekly); Signed Inside (10 s macro); Plainly (60-90 s honest answers). No prices, no emoji, no "we're so excited", no influencer seeding beyond a few trusted locals |
| Pinterest | Where bridal and gifting are researched | Every design and version pinned once, linked to its page |

| Appointments and in-store events | Where the house actually sells | Appointment omnipresent on the site; trunk evenings by invitation; a release evening for each Royal American piece; the bench open on a regular Saturday |
| Local press and partners | Earned trust | Nashville Lifestyles, StyleBlueprint, Williamson Herald; wedding planners and venues; stylists for artists **[verify]** contacts |
| Bridal | The largest single decision we serve | The builder as the bridal landing page; fittings by appointment; both stones shown side by side in the salon, labeled; Knot data says about 7 in 10 want to see the ring in person (https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/10-useful-stats-knots-engagement/) |
| Reviews | Keep the 2,537 honest and growing | Ask every repair and purchase customer; never incentivize or gate by sentiment (FTC 16 CFR 465 via research/04); show one dated line on store pages only |
| Paid search | Capture intent we cannot yet rank for | Brand plus local bridal and repair terms; builder and store pages as landing pages; no prices in ads except "From" for bridal. Needs Ads access |
| Paper | Be the thing worth keeping | A printed house book mailed twice a year to Brentwood, Franklin, Green Hills and Mt. Juliet zip codes |

Budget split in the playbook (Search 35, Meta 25, Pinterest 10, paper 10, press 10, events 10 percent) is a starting hypothesis only. Total budget: **Unknown**.

## Clienteling (Proposed)

A client book: birthdays, anniversaries, children, pieces owned, birthstones. Every repair goes home cleaned, in a care card, with a look at what the bench is making. Releases go to the client list first. Houses like Cartier and Van Cleef sell through clienteling and invitation-only events (https://robbreport.com.au/?p=55447); treat "never discount" at those houses as industry practice, not a documented policy **[verify]**.

## What we never do (Decided unless noted)

Discounts, sales, coupons, percent-off, countdowns, popups, promo bars, "free" or percent banners, financing banners, urgency, emoji, exclamation points, stock or AI imagery, models who are not the family, flags or eagles, comparative or environmental claims, "Made in America", "handmade", counting the lines, reviews on product pages, paid influencer campaigns (Proposed), chasing likes as a metric (Proposed).

## Measurement (Proposed)

- Appointments per week, and how many came from the site.
- Calls and direction requests from each GBP; local pack position for the core terms.
- Design codes created, pieces saved, bag adds, checkouts.
- Share of revenue from house designs versus vendor pieces, monthly.
- Average ticket; Family collection share of repeat purchases.
- Client list size and email replies (not opens).
- Reviews that mention the bench, the designer, the drawing or the mark.
Never report likes or follower counts as success.
