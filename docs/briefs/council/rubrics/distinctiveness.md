# Seat D: distinctiveness

The seat asks: with the name hidden, would anyone know this is the house, and does the screen do one romantic thing on purpose?

| ID | Rule | Check | Severity | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Hide the name. Cover the wordmark in the 1440 screenshot and say, in one sentence, what a reader would recognize and whether it could belong to another jeweler. | judged | Should if "any jeweler" | research/17 §2 (Romaniuk's hide-the-name test) |
| D2 | At least two of the house's distinctive assets appear on the page: the type on the paper, the registry grammar "No. 003" and a place name, the navy velvet (Royal American and the bag only), the provenance line verbatim, the hand frame, "the stone is set", the bench. | judged; list which appear | Should | research/17 §2 |
| D3 | The screen uses one mechanic of romance on purpose, and names it: pacing, withholding, naming, provenance and time, the letter, one sentence on a full screen, light, the object as protagonist, ritual, the person, silence, truth kept, the ending. | judged | Should if none can be named | research/17 §1 |
| D4 | The generic test: name the one part of the screen you would have produced for any jewelry brief, and the change that makes it this house's. | judged | Should; a page that is generic throughout is a Blocker | research/16 §2–3; frontend-design calibration |
| D5 | No AI tells: tracked caps eyebrows, middle-dot meta strings, arrow links, 01/02/03 markers, fade-up everywhere, icon cards, ornaments, equal three-card rows, cream plus a high-contrast serif with no other idea. | judged + lint | Blocker for each tell | canon AI-tell lint; research/10; frontend-design calibration |
| D6 | The page ends with a place or an invitation, never a sale. | judged | Should | research/17 §1 (the ending) |
| D7 | Quiet signals over loud ones: no logo repetition, no slogans, the mark inside the piece rather than on the page. | judged | Should | Han, Nunes & Drèze (2010); Berger & Ward (2010); research/18 §B |
| D8 | Structure is familiar, detail is not: a first-time visitor knows what the page is and what to do within a second, and nothing in the photography, type or copy is a template default. | judged | Should | Tuch et al. (2012): low complexity plus high prototypicality; research/18 §A |

Two questions close the seat, answered in one line each: *Would a person forward this screen to someone they love?* *Is anything here hurrying me?* (research/17 §1)
