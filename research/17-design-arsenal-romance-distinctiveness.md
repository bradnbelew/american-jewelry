# 17. The design arsenal: romance, distinctiveness, and everything that brings the site to life

**For:** the owner and the designer of American Jewelry, and the Claude Code sessions that build the site.
**Last updated:** 2026-10-11.
**What this is:** the design-only compendium. The owner's words: design, web design, branding, marketing, appeal, romance, cleanness, high-end feel, designer portrayal of the site, the brand and the products, the use of the website, and making it memorable, distinct, unique and stunning. Research/08 gave the direction, /10 the technical arsenal, /11 the devices of a house, /14 the images, /15 the classics. This file adds what was still missing: the mechanics of romance, distinctiveness as a system, the teachers, the galleries, the repos and tools for craft, the flows that must feel like a house, and a ranked list of ideas that belong only to this house.
**Method:** items marked ✓ were checked this session by search; the rest are long-standing resources cited by their well-known address and should be opened before being relied on. Nothing here repeats 10 §2–5 or 15 except by pointer.

## 0. How to use this file

- Read §1 and §2 once; they are the theory of feel and of memory. Everything else is a shelf to pull from.
- When a screen feels flat, go to §1 and ask which mechanic is missing. When a screen feels generic, go to §2 and ask which distinctive asset is absent.
- When learning, pick one teacher from §3 and one book from §4 per month. When stuck for composition, open three galleries from §5 with the "look for" column in mind, never for copying.
- When building, §6 and §7 are the toolbox; §8 is the list of flows that most sites get wrong; §9 is the house's own idea bank.

## 1. What romance is made of (the mechanics, with evidence and our version)

Romance on a luxury site is not softness or script type. It is a set of mechanics that make an object feel wanted, rare and cared for. Each one below names where it is done well and how this house does it.

| Mechanic | What it is | Seen at | Ours |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pacing** | Alternating scale: a macro that fills the screen, then a wide still, then white. The eye breathes. | Apple product pages; Cartier 365 chapters (10 §5); Hermès campaign pages | Home: macro of the design of the season → the three names as type → the hand → the bench film. Never two macros in a row. |
| **Withholding** | What is not shown or priced becomes the thing most wanted. | "Price on demand" (Boucheron), "Price upon request" (David Webb), by-appointment salons (11 §8) | Royal American: price on request, seen in person, entered in a registry. The salon is a room, not a page. |
| **Naming** | A name turns a SKU into a character. | Alighieri names every piece after a canto of Dante and calls them "modern heirlooms" ✓ ([AnOther](https://www.anothermag.com/fashion-beauty/13660/myths-magic-and-medievalism-the-bewitching-rise-of-alighieri)); Sophie Bille Brahe's Archive; VCA's Alhambra origin story | Designs (Shooting Star, Prism, Signal, Grid), Royal American pieces named for Tennessee places with a registry number. Never "Style #AJ-1042". |
| **Provenance and time** | A date, a place, a maker, a drawing. Objects with histories are loved; objects without them are compared on price. | Auction lot pages: maker, date, provenance by sale and lot (15 §4); Patek's archive extracts | The provenance line, the drawing beneath the ring (11 §7), "Set in Brentwood", the registry's "Acquired" line, the certificate at `/design/:code/certificate`. |
| **The letter** | First person, signed, no selling. | Brunello Cucinelli's philosophy sentence on the hero (10 §5); Patek's generations line | The House letter, signed by first names, as the last screen of the home and the whole of /house. |
| **One sentence on a full screen** | The confidence to let eight words own a viewport. | Lusion, Brunello Cucinelli (10 §5) | Line pages: eyebrow in sentence case, the name, one sentence, the hand frame. |
| **Light** | Daylight on skin; fire in a stone that answers the hand. | Rolex's pale studio gradient (06); the drei refraction demo (08 §4) | Pointer-reactive key light on every 3D view; daylight-only hand photography (14 §2). |
| **Sound** | Natural sound, never music; off by default with a visible control. | Hermès video with real play/pause (10 §5); Patek's visible motion toggle | The 12-second bench loop with the laser's tick and the polishing wheel, a "Sound" control, no soundtrack anywhere. |
| **The object as protagonist** | The piece is staged in a story, not displayed on a shelf. | Leïla Menchari's 136–137 Hermès windows, four a year, each a theatre set built around objects ✓ ([Fondation Azzedine Alaïa](https://fondationazzedinealaia.org/en/shop/leila-menchari-la-reine-mage/), [AnOther](https://anothermag.com/fashion-beauty/10351/the-fascinating-woman-behind-hermes-window-displays)) | Four "design of the season" takeovers a year: one film, one letter, one macro. The store windows follow the same four stories. |
| **Ritual** | Repeated, unhurried care that costs the house something. | Tiffany's complimentary cleaning by appointment; Cartier Care; Rolex service intervals (research/13 §4) | Free cleaning and inspection forever, written as a ritual ("Fifteen minutes. Bring it in."), a six-month note that sells nothing. |
| **The person** | A named designer, the family's hands, a bench you can watch. | Tacori's glass wall to the workshop; Todd Reed's glass-walled shop; Tiffany, Yurman, Garber name the designer (research/13 §2) | The brother named on every design page; the family's hands in every hand frame; the open bench as the most-photographed thing we own. |
| **Silence** | No promos, no urgency, no counters. The absence is the signal. | Houses email about once a week at 0 % promotion (research/13 §4) | The canon's never-do list; one email a week at most; holiday is wrapping and a letter. |
| **Truth kept** | An inclusion left in the photograph; "imported" said plainly; a report you can read. | Sotheby's "assumed treated unless stated natural"; Alighieri's "imperfection at its core" ✓ | The folded stone report; the provenance line; the New American standard stated in grader's words. |
| **The ending** | Every page ends with a place or an invitation, never a sale. | Aman's single "Reserve" (10 §5) | Every page ends with the two addresses and "Come and see it." |

Two tests for romance on any screen: *Would a person forward this to someone they love?* and *Is anything here hurrying me?*

## 2. Distinctiveness as a system (how a house becomes memorable)

Jenni Romaniuk's *Building Distinctive Brand Assets* (Oxford, 2018 ✓, [OUP](https://www.oup.com.au/books/higher-education/management-and-marketing/9780190311506-building-distinctive-brand-assets)) measures a brand's non-name signals on two axes: **fame** (what share of category buyers link the asset to the brand) and **uniqueness** (how far the brand owns it against rivals), plotted on a Distinctive Asset Grid. The practical test, used by Marketing Week on a Müller ad ✓ ([Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/overconfidence-in-distinctive-brand-assets/)): hide the name, show the asset to category buyers, count who names the brand. Byron Sharp's *How Brands Grow* (OUP) is the parent argument: consistency of a few assets over years beats novelty. Kapferer disagrees with Sharp on almost everything except this.

**The house's distinctive assets (choose five, use them everywhere for ten years):**
1. The star hallmark struck inside the shank (and, on screen, the hallmark frame).
2. Libre Caslon on paper `#F5F2EC` (the Declaration's type on a warm ground; 15 §6).
3. The registry number grammar: "No. 003" and a Tennessee place name.
4. The navy velvet `#16213A` reserved for Royal American and the bag.
5. The provenance line, verbatim, on every piece.
6. The hand frame: the family's hand, daylight, one piece.
7. "The stone is set": the assembly as the house's single motion.
8. The bench film with natural sound.

Rules: the same five appear on the site, the box, the card in the box, the email header, the Instagram grid, the store window and the appointment letter. Do the hide-the-name test with ten customers twice a year. Never redesign an asset that is gaining fame; refine it.

## 3. Teachers: courses, blogs and streams worth the hours

**Motion and interaction**
- Emil Kowalski, animations.dev (10 §4) and his essays. The theory of restraint.
- Josh Comeau, *Whimsical Animations* ✓ ([whimsy.joshwcomeau.com](https://whimsy.joshwcomeau.com/)): a hands-on course across DOM, canvas, SVG and scroll-driven animation; launched 2025–2026. Also his free "CSS transitions" and "Designing beautiful shadows" essays. Use for the few moments that answer a click.
- Rauno Freiberg, rauno.me/craft (10 §4). The standard for invisible details.
- Val Head, *Designing Interface Animation* (Rosenfeld) and Rachel Nabors, *Animation at Work* (A Book Apart): choreography and purpose. IBM Carbon's motion guidance (productive vs expressive, with easing tokens) is the clearest free reference for duration and easing choices.

**3D and shaders**
- Bruno Simon, *Three.js Journey* (threejs-journey.com): the canonical course; the shaders and R3F chapters are what the ring needs.
- Maxime Heckel's blog (blog.maximeheckel.com): refraction, dispersion, caustics and post-processing written for React Three Fiber; the closest public writing to our stone.
- Yuri Artiukh ("akella"), YouTube streams reconstructing award sites in three.js; watch how he reads a reference, not what he builds.
- Codrops (tympanus.net/codrops) tutorials and their GitHub demos (10 §2): read for technique, never for the look.

**Design craft**
- Google Fonts Knowledge ✓ ([fonts.google.com/knowledge](https://fonts.google.com/knowledge)): free, 70+ lessons on choosing, pairing and using type with a glossary; the fastest typographic education for the family ([Material blog](https://m2.material.io/blog/google-fonts-knowledge)).
- Erik Kennedy, Learn UI Design: the free articles ("7 Rules for Creating Gorgeous UI", on color, on type) are the best plain-English hierarchy teaching online; the paid course is thorough.
- Shift Nudge (Matt D. Smith): interface design fundamentals taught through critique.
- Growth.Design case studies and Laws of UX (Jon Yablonski, also a book): the psychology behind the flows in §8.
- Smashing Magazine's editorial track, especially Andy Clarke's *Art Direction for the Web* ✓ (2019; print and ebook, [Smashing](https://smashingmagazine.com/printed-books/art-direction-for-the-web)): the argument for art-directing web pages like Brodovitch and Feitler art-directed Harper's Bazaar. The chapters on when and why, not just how, are the brief for our design pages.

**Studios' own writing (case studies as lessons)**
- 14islands, Immersive Garden, makemepulse, Instrument (Oura), Locomotive, Darkroom Engineering (10 §5).
- basement studio's blog and open source (see §6): how a studio builds scroll stories for commercial clients without hijacking scroll.
- Lusion, Resn, Active Theory, Hello Monday: open for ambition, not for patterns; they are showreels, we are a shop (10 §5).

## 4. Books not already on the shelf (brand, emotion, editorial, jewelry)

The classics are in research/15. These are the ones about memory, emotion and staging.

**Brand and memory**
- Marty Neumeier, *The Brand Gap* and *Zag* (New Riders): "a brand is a person's gut feeling"; radical differentiation in one sentence each.
- Alina Wheeler, *Designing Brand Identity* (Wiley): the process book; the chapters on touchpoints and launch.
- Jenni Romaniuk, *Building Distinctive Brand Assets* (OUP) ✓ and Byron Sharp, *How Brands Grow* (OUP): §2.
- Debbie Millman, *Brand Thinking and Other Noble Pursuits* (Allworth): interviews; read Neumeier's and Wheeler's.
- Kevin Roberts, *Lovemarks* (powerHouse): mystery, sensuality, intimacy as the three sources of loyalty beyond respect. Dated in tone, useful as a checklist for romance.
- Pine & Gilmore, *The Experience Economy* (HBR Press): why the bench you can watch is the product.

**Emotion and story**
- Don Norman, *Emotional Design* (Basic Books): visceral, behavioral, reflective; the registry is reflective design.
- Aarron Walter, *Designing for Emotion* (A Book Apart): personality in interfaces without whimsy.
- Ellen Lupton, *Design Is Storytelling* (Cooper Hewitt): narrative arc, sensory detail and the "gutter" between frames, applied to design.
- Frank Chimero, *The Shape of Design* (free online at shapeofdesign.com): why and how, not what; the chapter on improvisation and limits.
- Gaston Bachelard, *The Poetics of Space* (Beacon): drawers, chests and the intimacy of small enclosed places. Read before designing the bag drawer, the box and the registry.
- Leonard Koren, *Wabi-Sabi for Artists, Designers, Poets & Philosophers* (Imperfect Publishing): the aesthetics of truth kept.
- Alan Fletcher, *The Art of Looking Sideways* (Phaidon): a lifetime of visual thinking; open at random.

**Editorial and layout**
- Andy Clarke, *Art Direction for the Web* ✓ (Smashing).
- Timothy Samara, *Making and Breaking the Grid* (Rockport) and Kimberly Elam, *Typographic Systems* and *Geometry of Design* (Princeton Architectural Press): the asymmetric systems behind 7/5 splits.
- Kinfolk and Cereal magazines; Monocle: the editorial restraint the site borrows (one image, generous margins, captions that inform).

**Jewelry and the houses (monographs with photography worth studying frame by frame)**
- *Leïla Menchari: La Reine Mage* (Actes Sud/Hermès, 2017; 408 pp; 137 windows) ✓ ([Fondation Azzedine Alaïa](https://fondationazzedinealaia.org/en/shop/leila-menchari-la-reine-mage/)): the single best book on staging objects as stories.
- *Jewels by JAR* (Metropolitan Museum): photography on black, no copy; the opposite ground, the same discipline.
- *The Cartier Collection: Jewelry* (Flammarion); *Van Cleef & Arpels: The Art and Science of Gems* (Thames & Hudson); *Harry Winston: Rare Jewels of the World* (Rizzoli); *Bulgari: Roman Treasures* (Rizzoli); *Vogue: The Jewellery* (Conran). Study how each house photographs scale, metal and stone, and what the captions say.
- The American lineage monographs are in 15 §8 (Webb, Verdura, Schepps, Heyman, Tiffany, Rainwater).

## 6. Repos and tools for craft (beyond research/10)

**Scroll and sequence**
- `basementstudio/scrollytelling` ✓ ([docs](https://scrollytelling.basement.studio/docs), [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/basementstudio/scrollytelling/main/README.md)): React components over GSAP ScrollTrigger with position-based progress (`Root`, `Animation`, `Waypoint`, `Parallax`, `ImageSequenceCanvas`), a Lenis example and a three.js example. The `ImageSequenceCanvas` is exactly the fallback in 14 §4.4. Peer-depends on GSAP.
- GSAP (10 §2) with ScrollTrigger and SplitText now free; `@gsap/react` is already installed.
- Lenis, Satus, Hamo, Tempus from Darkroom (10 §5).

**3D pipeline**
- `pmndrs/gltfjsx`: turns a GLB into a typed React component so the ring's parts (band, head, stone, four claws) become named JSX nodes you can animate individually; the companion to the Blender-authored clip in 14 §4.3.
- `pmndrs/leva`: a tweak panel for calibrating light, roughness and IOR on the live ring during development; strip it from production.
- `pmndrs/maath`: `damp` for the scroll scrub (14 §4.3).
- `gkjohnson/three-mesh-bvh`: the dependency behind drei's refraction material; read its README before upgrading three.
- `donmccurdy/glTF-Transform` (already a dev dependency): Draco, KTX2, dedupe, prune.
- Khronos glTF Sample Viewer and Blender: inspect the GLB exactly as a browser will light it.

**Layout, type, color, easing**
- Open Props ✓ ([open-props.style](https://open-props.style/)): Adam Argyle's CSS custom-property tokens; import only `easings.min.css` for a vetted set of ease-out, ease-in-out and spring-like curves to compare against `--ease-house`. Also exports Design Tokens 2025.10 JSON.
- Every Layout, Utopia, Capsize (15 §5, 10 §4).
- VisBug (Chrome extension by Argyle): nudge spacing, swap type and inspect any live page, including Hermès and Cartier, to learn their numbers by touching them.
- Wakamai Fondue (wakamaifondue.com): drop a font file in, see every OpenType feature; use it to find Libre Caslon's small caps, old-style and lining figures, ligatures.
- Color: oklch.com for perceptual steps; Leonardo (Adobe) for contrast-based scales; APCA calculator (apcacontrast.com) and Who Can Use (whocanuse.com) for the gold-on-paper question (DESIGN.md lint: 2.77:1).
- Easing references: easings.net, cubic-bezier.com.
- Responsively (open source) or Polypane (paid): all breakpoints side by side while you work.

**Sketch to screen**
- tldraw and its "make real" experiment: draw a layout, get HTML; a faster way to communicate composition to Claude Code than words. Excalidraw for wireframes. Penpot if you want an open-source Figma.
- Claude Design, Figma MCP: 16 §6.
- Rive (interactive vector animation with state machines) and Spline (3D with React export) are capable tools we do not need; Unicorn Studio and Jitter likewise. Listed so no one adds them for a hero effect.

**Images and OG**
- `vercel/satori` (OG images already built this way), Squoosh, SVGOMG, Shopify CDN params (07).

## 7. Type beyond the three we use

We ship Libre Caslon Display, Libre Caslon Text and Public Sans (the story in 15 §6). If a second voice is ever needed for print or a campaign, look here before anywhere else and never add a fourth web face.
- Free for commercial use: Fontshare (Indian Type Foundry; check the current ITF Free Font License before use), Google Fonts, Velvetyne, Open Foundry, Collletttivo, The League of Moveable Type.

## 8. The use of the website: flows that must feel like a house

Most luxury sites fail here (Baymard: six of seven mediocre; NN/g: failure in the consider phase). Each flow below is where the template reasserts itself unless designed.

1. **Appointment.** No calendar widget chrome. A short form (name, store, a day and a time of day, what to bring), confirmed by a letter-shaped email and a printed card at the counter. If a scheduler is needed, self-hosted or white-labeled so no third-party brand appears. The word is "Visit" or "Private viewing", never "Book a consultation".
2. **Saving.** "Keep" a piece into a drawer (Bachelard), not a heart into a wishlist. The drawer page is paper, the pieces in a single column with their names.
3. **The builder.** One ring on screen throughout; a price ledger, not a price; the engraving read live; the finished design gets a code and a certificate page you can send. "Build on Signal", never "Customize".
4. **The bag.** The navy drawer; a photograph of the box under "What arrives"; one honesty line about tax; no upsell row.
5. **Checkout.** The one page we do not fully own. On non-Plus plans the checkout editor sets logo, colors and fonts; the full Checkout Branding API (type sizes, components) requires Plus ✓ ([Shero](https://sherocommerce.com/blogs/insights/how-to-customize-the-shopify-checkout-without-plus), [Shopify docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/2026-07/objects/CheckoutBranding)); since May 2026 the branding editor also covers customer accounts and sign-in ([changelog](https://changelog.shopify.com/posts/draft-unified-branding-customization-across-checkout-and-customer-accounts)). Set paper, ink, Caslon where allowed and test that the handoff does not feel like a fall.
6. **Email.** One a week at most, 0 % promotion (research/13 §4). Designed as a letter: one image, one Caslon line, one link, the two addresses. The six-month cleaning note sells nothing.
7. **Search.** Small catalog, strong filters by design, metal, stone; no search box on the home page (Baymard: luxury search is the weakest area, so remove the need for it).
8. **The 404.** A drawing and one sentence: "This page is not in the registry." Then the two addresses.
9. **Share.** "Show someone": a quiet link that opens the piece page as it is, with the Open Graph image already built (07).
10. **Accessibility as luxury.** Visible focus, 16px inputs, reduced motion honored, real contrast: the quality floor from research/10 and the frontend-design skill, which also happens to be how the houses lose points at Baymard.

## 9. Ideas that belong only to this house (ranked; new, not repeating 08 §4)

1. **Four windows a year.** Menchari's model: four seasonal stories, each a "design of the season" with one film, one macro, one letter, mirrored in both store windows. The site and the street tell the same story.
2. **The week it was made.** A product-page line for made-to-order pieces: "Set in Brentwood the week before it ships. Your initials inside." Time is provenance.
3. **The registry as a living page.** Royal American one-of-ones listed by number and place name, with "Acquired" and the year; the certificate route already exists. Over time it becomes the house's archive and the reason to return.
4. **The House letter as the home's last screen.** Signed by first names. The only first-person voice on the site.
5. **The origin journal.** Montana sapphire and Tennessee freshwater pearl as journal entries written with the farm's and the mine's names (research/12 §4), with the stone's own photograph. Americana that is true.
6. **The type story paragraph** on the House page (Caslon and the Declaration; Public Sans and Franklin): one paragraph, no boast.
7. **The box in the bag.** A photograph of the box and the card with the registry number under "What arrives." The offline distinctive assets shown before purchase.
8. **The cleaning ritual note.** Six months after a purchase, a letter-shaped email: "Bring it in. Fifteen minutes." No link to shop.
9. **Natural sound, visible control.** The bench film carries the laser's tick; a "Sound" word toggles it; no music anywhere on the site.
10. **The appointment card.** The confirmation printed on the house paper at the counter, Caslon, the registry grammar, kept by the customer.
11. **The drawing beneath** (11 §7) and **the hallmark frame** (08 §4): already specified; listed here because they are the two most romantic things already in the plan.
12. **A small printed lookbook** designed as the site is designed (same Caslon, same paper, same photographs), left in both stores and sent to the fifty best customers. Print makes a web identity feel inevitable.

Not recommended, again: cursor effects, parallax on photographs, animated motifs, music, AR, true scale, pop-ups asking for an email, review stars, countdowns.

## 10. A thirty-day design practice for the owner

- **Week 1, numbers.** With VisBug and the CSS Overview panel, measure four sites (Hermès, Aesop, Leica, Hemmerle): type sizes, measures, gaps, image aspect ratios, words above the fold. Write them beside ours.
- **Week 2, words.** Copy twenty auction lot descriptions and twenty Hermès product sentences by hand (15 §4). Rewrite the House letter and five product sentences.
- **Week 3, images.** Build a 40-image board on Are.na or Cosmos with one written reason per image. Cull to 12. Those 12 are the photographer's brief (14 §6) and the designer's wall.
- **Week 4, one screen three ways.** With `/house`, build the product page top as three complete variants from one brief and one sketch; run the critic; pick one; delete two; commit.

Repeat monthly with a different screen. The site becomes a house one screen at a time.
