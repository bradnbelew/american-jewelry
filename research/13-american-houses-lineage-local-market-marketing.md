## 0. Decisions on one screen

1. **Join the lineage as a member, not a claimant (§1).** The American house is a documented tradition: Boston 1796, New York 1810, Tiffany 1837, Oscar Heyman 1912, Winston 1932, Verdura 1939, Webb 1948. What the survivors share is own designs, own workshop, a named person. We have the workshop and the designs; the named person is the family's decision (§2.3, §7).
2. **Earn "house" with facts; never use "first" or "oldest" (§1.2, §6).** Black, Starr & Frost owns "America's First Jeweler" [3]; Shreve, Crump & Low owns "America's Jeweler since 1796" [37]; Tiffany owns the American-luxury origin story [39]. Our sentence is about Tennessee and the bench.
3. **Motif names are usable (§1.3).** No live USPTO Class 14 registration surfaced for SHOOTING STAR, SIGNAL or GRID; two SHOOTING STAR and one SIGNAL applications were abandoned [43][44][50]; PRISM has applications of unconfirmed status [46][47]. The collisions are reputational: Ole Lynggaard's "Shooting Stars" [45], Boodles' "Prism" [48], Fernando Jorge's "Signal" ring [51], Kinley's "Grid" [52]. Always pair the motif with the house name; ask counsel about intent-to-use filings **[verify]**.
4. **Every family-to-house case turned on one move (§2):** a signature form (Tacori crescent, Yurman Cable, Verragio Lumino), a material stance (Todd Reed rough diamonds, Single Stone old cuts, Lika Behar 24k), a visible workshop (Tacori's glass window, Todd Reed's glass-walled shop, Šepkus's bench), or a named succession (Garber, Kirk Kara, Tacori). We already have the visible workshop; the signature is the work ahead.
5. **Name the designer (§2.3).** Tiffany built chapters on Schlumberger, Peretti, Picasso and Cummings [54][55]; Tacori names Nadine Tacorian as Head of Design [56]; Yurman is the person [68]. Cartier and VCA keep design anonymous because designers leave; a family house with one designer should name him, picture him at the bench, and print his name on the certificate.
6. **The market is richer and emptier than it looks (§3).** Williamson County median household income $135,600 (2020-2024), highest of Tennessee's 95 counties; Wilson County second at $95,800 [92][97]. Hermès (2025), Saint Laurent, Louboutin, Gucci arrived [101][103][106]. No Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels, Harry Winston or Bulgari boutique exists in Tennessee [153]. Two Official Rolex Retailers: Diamond Cellar Nashville (2020) and Fink's Franklin (2023) [110][111].
7. **The open position (§3.4):** nobody in Middle Tennessee designs its own pieces, sets them on a bench the client can watch, and sells them at house prices in its own stores. The local set is multi-brand luxury retail (King, Diamond Cellar, Fink's, Genesis), national boutiques (Tiffany, Yurman, Brilliant Earth), estate (Walton's), and maker studios under $1,000 or custom-only (Judith Bright, Consider the Wldflwrs, ME, Brooke Worthington).
8. **Pre-owned Rolex wording (§3.3):** "Pre-owned Rolex, by appointment. We are not an Official Rolex Retailer." Rolex's directory lists Diamond Cellar and Fink's [110][111]; King Jewelers' 2017 "only authorized dealer" claim does not appear there [119] **[verify]**.
9. **Market like the houses (§4):** "book an appointment" and "private salon" language [153][157][159]; a care lane that brings people back (Tiffany: complimentary cleaning by appointment, up to five pieces [162]; Cartier Care: warranty extension by registration [164]; Rolex: service about every ten years, two-year service guarantee [166][168]); about one email a week with zero promotions (Tiffany 1.37/wk, Cartier 1.35/wk, VCA 1.00/wk, all 0% promotional) [190][191][192].
10. **Holiday without a sale (§4.6):** Cartier's end-of-year page is wishlists for her and him, personalization, extended returns to 15 January, complimentary express delivery; no price is cut [194][195]. Ours: a gift page, engraving, wrapping, extended returns, nothing else.
11. **Launch like a house at our scale (§4.7):** private evening plus press preview plus a film or a book plus a travelling exhibition is the pattern (Tiffany Landmark 27 April 2023 [172]; Cartier Trinity 100 [174][175]; VCA opens stores with an exhibition that runs for months [171]). Ours: a letter, an evening at the Brentwood bench, a three-minute film, a printed book, Mt. Juliet four weeks later.
12. **Measure the house, not the shop (§5):** appointments, walk-ins attributed, GBP calls/directions/clicks, review velocity, repair-to-purchase, average ticket, email replies. Benchmarks: Edge 2024 average ticket +7% [205]; INSTORE 2025 modal average ticket $500-$1,499 [211]; JA historical gross margin 48-49% [214][215]; shop services 6-25% of a jeweler's business [216].

## 1. The American house lineage

### 1.1 What "house" meant in America

In the American record the firms remembered as houses had four things: a person's name on the door (or a designer whose name the firm signed), designs made for the firm rather than bought from a catalog, a workshop the firm controlled, and a clientele that could be named. Age, Fifth Avenue, silver and trophies were secondary; Marcus & Co. and Trabert & Hoeffer had all of the first three and vanished when the making stopped [4][9]. Of the fifteen names below, the ones still making in the United States today are Tiffany, Oscar Heyman, Harry Winston, Verdura, Seaman Schepps and David Webb; every one of those six still runs or names a New York workshop [12][16][17][21][23][26]. The retailers that survived without a workshop (Shreve & Co., Shreve Crump & Low, Black, Starr & Frost) survived as stores, and their sites sell the year, not the bench [3][30][37].

### 1.3 The twentieth-century American motif vocabulary and who owns what

The American vocabulary, as the record shows it: stars and celestial forms (Yard, Winston's cluster), skyscraper and machine geometry (Trabert & Hoeffer's cast Reflection elements) [9], animals with a joke in them (Yard's rabbits, Schlumberger's bird, Webb's bestiary) [6][38][24], shells and found materials (Schepps) [21], and Americana without a flag: the registered number on a Tiffany tag, the West Point ring, the military medals BB&B designed [40][2][31]. Motifs are free; specific expressions are owned or defended.

## 2. Family jeweler to house: cases with evidence

### 2.1 The cases

**Tacori (Glendale, California; 1979).** Haig and Gilda Tacorian, Romanian Armenians; pearls first, diamonds in the early 1990s, bridal by 2005; the crescent silhouette grew from a 1998 engagement commission; The Bachelorette drove demand; the children run it, Paul as Chairman and Nadine as Head of Design [56][57][58]. The move: one silhouette, an American-making claim on every page, and sales only through authorized retailers ("Tacori does not sell directly to the public"), now with a 2025 shop-in-shop program [59][60]. Site: "At Tacori Design Studio in Glendale, a glass window separates the offices from the workshop" [56]; the story is one long scroll of names and dates. Weak: the product grid is a wholesale catalog with style numbers. Lesson: a glass wall is a brand claim; our bench is already on the floor (09 §2), so photograph and film it as Tacori does.

**Single Stone (Los Angeles; showroom 1988, first collection 1998 or 2004 depending on source).** Ari Madilian dealt modern diamonds, moved to old cuts; Corina joined as partner; the collection is made in the downtown LA jewelry district "by master craftsmen using antique tools and old hand-cut diamonds"; wholesale first, then their own multi-brand store in San Marino in 2006 [61][62][63][64]. The move: a material stance, old European and old mine cuts, so that "each piece begins with the center stone" [61]. Weak (11 §6): an Affirm bar. Lesson: a house can be defined by what it refuses to set; ours refuses stone inventory and sells set stones at set sizes, man-made diamond stated plainly.

**Sidney Garber (Chicago, 1946; now also New York).** A watch assembler in the Mallers Building asked for pearls and rings, then a Michigan Avenue boutique; died 2008; daughter Brooke Garber Neidich became creative director, opened on Madison Avenue, joined the CFDA in 2015, gives the profits away; 75th anniversary 2021 [65][66]. The move: the second generation became the designer and the face. Lesson: succession is a brand event only if the successor designs. (Site in 08 §2.)

**David Yurman (New York, 1980).** Sculptor and painter; first Cable between 1978 and 1983 depending on source; first boutique 1999; the Green Hills store is one of 49 wholly owned, and the Yurmans visit several times a year [67][68][69][109]. The move: one form for forty years, and a couple as the face. Lesson: the signature has to survive at every price, Cable in silver and in diamonds; ours must work as a stud and as a signet.

**Todd Reed (Boulder; first shop 1996, Pearl Street showroom and studio 2010).** Self-taught; rough diamonds from collector Jack Greenspan; "each piece is made from start to finish within the walls of his studio, by one single jeweller"; 16 jewelers at peak in a glass-walled workshop, up to fifty pieces a month; now commissions only, per one profile [70][71][72][73]. The move: a material nobody wanted plus a workshop you can see from the showroom. Lesson: a mountain town became part of the name because the making was the show.

**Alex Šepkus (New York, 1991).** Vilnius-born; repairs at Julius Cohen; a $10,000 start with partner Jeff Feero; JA New Designer 1993; gold engraved, then textured under magnification; the firm builds its own electric hammers; the finisher "has the last say"; a workshop nicknamed "the U.N." with 20-to-33-year tenures; Šepkus died in 2021 and the archive continues [74][75][76]. The move: a technique visible at 10x, with the maker's name as the brand even after his death. Lesson: write down and photograph the bench's own methods (our laser welding, our setting) as the house's method, not "state of the art".

**Verragio (New York, 1996).** Apprenticed at 14, FIT, the Lumino setting invented for his girlfriend's ring [77][78]. The move: one proprietary-looking setting plus a founder love story. Lesson: the first piece's story sells for thirty years; ask the family which piece was first (§7).

**Kirk Kara (Los Angeles; lineage 1890 Kayseri, Beirut after 1915, LA in the 1980s).** Hand engraving as the signature; renamed from "Something Special" to "Kirk Kara" when the daughters joined; Angela Karaguezian CEO 2020 [79][80][81]. The move: the father's name on the door and the ancestral technique kept. Lesson: "American Jewelry" is a category name, so the designer's name must do the work inside it (§2.3).

**Erica Courtney (Los Angeles; Dallas start, 1984).** Colored stones before the market wanted them; the Drop Dead Gorgeous line; stylists and red carpets; stones sourced in the field with a gemologist [82][83]. Lesson: a colored-stone lane is a defensible second act for a diamond house; only if the family has a stone story to tell.

**Judith Ripka (New York, 1977).** Finlay leased departments, QVC from 1997, sixteen stores closed after 2008, sold to Xcel Brands for $22.5m, relaunches since [84][85][86]. The move that failed: scale through television. Lesson: a channel that discounts the name ends the house; it is the mirror image of our rule.

**Lika Behar (New York and Istanbul).** Banking, then childrenswear, then hammered 22k and 24k gold against oxidized silver; a family of Istanbul gold dealers [87][88]. Lesson: a material signature can carry a house without a famous founder; ours could be a declared alloy or finish on every piece.

**John Hardy (Bali, 1975; Mambal workshop 1996).** Canadian designer with Balinese silversmiths, about 700 artisans; private equity in 2007, L Catterton in 2014 on about $75m sales [89][90][91]. Lesson: the workshop as destination (tours, meals) built the brand and outlived the founder. Ours is 157 Franklin Road; let people in.

### 2.3 "The designer": named or anonymous

Tiffany signs Schlumberger, Peretti, Picasso and Cummings and tells its history through them [54][55]. Tacori names Nadine Tacorian as Head of Design on the story page [56]. Yurman is David and Sybil [68]. Sidney Garber is Brooke [65]. Todd Reed and Alex Šepkus are the firm's name [70][74]. Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels and Harry Winston present design as the house's own, with no designer named on the pages checked in 01; corporate houses hide designers because designers leave. A family house with one designer has the opposite problem: anonymity reads as a catalog. Decision: the brother is named on the House page and on every design page ("Designed by [first name last name], [year]"), photographed at the bench and not in a suit, and his name printed on the certificate with the piece number, date, metal and the stone statement (natural or man-made). The old site's "our Master Jewelers" and "Jewelry Stylists" [151] become first names (09 §2).

### 3.4 The open position

The set in one line each: multi-brand luxury retailers that sell other people's designs (King, Diamond Cellar, Fink's, Genesis); national boutiques (Tiffany, Yurman, Brilliant Earth); estate (Walton's); maker studios under $1,000 or custom-only (Bright, Wldflwrs, ME, Worthington). Nobody is a fine-jewelry house that designs its own pieces, makes them on a bench the client can watch, and sells them at house prices in its own two stores. The three claims no one local is making, each true for us today per the old site and the brief [151]:

1. **"Designed here. Set here. You can watch."** The open studio is already the old site's most distinctive true fact (09 §2); Tacori sells the same idea through a glass window [56].
2. **"One designer, every piece."** No local store names a designer of all its pieces; Yurman does, from New York [109]. The brother's name is the claim.
3. **"2,537 reviews. Two stores. One bench."** The house canon dates the count to 2026-08-26 (5.0 Brentwood, 4.9 Mt. Juliet). No local independent surfaced a count near it: King 249 (StoneAlgo), Genesis 61 (The Knot) [123][129] **[verify Google counts for all three before publishing, and never publish it as a comparison, only as our number with its date]**.

## 4. How a house markets without discounting

### 4.1 Appointment language, exact words

- **Cartier** (store pages): "We recommend booking an appointment prior to your boutique visit, as walk-ins may experience extended wait times."; "To repair or customize your jewelry and timepieces, reserve an appointment with our experts."; Scottsdale lists "a private VIP Salon, a Diamond Salon" [153][155][156].
- **Van Cleef & Arpels:** the button is "BOOK AN APPOINTMENT"; Costa Mesa and Beverly Hills are "operating by appointment only ... we invite you to book your visit in advance"; services listed as "engraving, adjustment, and shining" [157][158].
- **Tiffany:** "book an appointment with your preferred store" from the store locator; "schedule an in-store consultation" with "a diamond expert"; virtual appointments "for product recommendations and styling or customer service and repair"; the title is "client advisor" [159][160][161].
- **Rolex directory:** the retailer label is "Official Rolex Retailer"; the Diamond Cellar page adds "offering Rolex Certified Pre-Owned watches" and "authorized to arrange servicing for your Rolex watch" [110][166] [snippet].
- **David Yurman Green Hills:** "A private viewing room is available by appointment" [109].

Our words (decision): "Book a visit" as the site's one call; "By appointment" for commissions, pre-owned Rolex and the private room; "the salon" only if a room exists; never "VIP"; "visit" rather than "consultation" wherever the canon's commission flow ("consult, CAD by email, approve") does not require the word.

### 4.2 Events and trunk shows

INSTORE's rules: plan about four months out; never invite two vendors who compete for the same dollars; use a trunk show to test a brand before committing [202]. Jewellery Business: match your energy to the client, pitch the stone or the story depending on who is in front of you, take notes, photograph only with permission [203]. Clientbook (a software vendor): build the list from purchase history, wishlists and anniversaries; keep each associate's personal text list to a few dozen; it claims appointment conversion 40-60% above walk-ins, unsourced [199][200]. JCK's clienteling feature covers the same ground from the retailer side [201]. For a house with no outside brands the trunk show becomes the designer's evening: new pieces from the bench, the brother present, nothing discounted. King's Wedding Weekend at 25% off is the local example of what we do not do [121].

### 4.3 Care and service as the retention engine

- **Tiffany:** "complimentary on-site cleanings for any Tiffany & Co. products by appointment"; "up to five items can be submitted per appointment"; a cleaning checks worn metal, stone security and prongs; polishing is a paid service; UK stores take no walk-in servicing [162][163].
- **Cartier Care:** watch coverage extended from two to eight years on registration (QR code on the warranty card or serial number online), with a full service, water-resistance check, battery and bracelet repair at dates the house sets [164][165].
- **Rolex:** the 2015 statement that the typical interval "has grown to approximately ten years"; Official Retailers arrange service; a two-year Service Guarantee after a complete service [166][167][168] [snippet].
- **Us:** the old warranty already promises free inspection and cleaning forever and publishes repair prices from $39 (09 §2). Decision: name the free service ("the annual inspection"), book it like Tiffany does, send a six-month reminder by email, and treat the repair counter as the acquisition channel it already is. Stuller's figures are a vendor's but directionally right: repairs close about 9 in 10, service customers are "almost 80% more likely to return" [217]; INSTORE's nine tips on converting repair clients are the operating manual [218].

### 4.5 The bridal channel

The Knot's Best of Weddings 2025 went to vendors with at least seven reviews averaging 4.5 or better in 2024 [184]; Genesis shows 4.5 from 61 reviews and an "Award winner (2x)" badge; King has a listing [129]. ### 4.6 Instagram, email and holiday

- **Instagram.** Luxury Society's Luxury Instagram Index (February 2026) ranks 70 watch and jewelry brands on engagement and growth rather than follower count [186]; its 2015 snapshot had Tiffany at 3.3m followers with 1.2% engagement and VCA sixth with the highest engagement of the ten [187]. Cadence benchmarks: L2's 2015 study of luxury fashion averaged ten posts a week [188]; Union Metrics found most brands post once or twice a day [189]. House cadence is inferred, not counted: a few posts a week, one subject per post, no prices, workshop and archive as recurring formats (Webb's "Just In from the Workshop", Schepps's "Image from our workshop making the ..." captions in 11 §1.4) **[verify by counting each house's feed for four weeks]**.
- **Email.** MailCharts 90-day averages: Tiffany 1.37 emails a week, 0% promotional; Cartier 1.35, 0%; Van Cleef & Arpels 1.00, 0%; Tory Burch 5.87 with 58% promotional [190][191][192][193]. Decision: one letter every two to four weeks, zero promotions, a subject line that names a piece or a person at the bench.
- **Holiday.** Cartier's end-of-year page is "The Cartier Gift": "Wishlist for Her", "Wishlist for Him", personalization ("Initials or engraved messages"), "free extended return & exchange until 15th January", complimentary express delivery; no price is reduced anywhere on it [194][195]. The logic is Kapferer's: keep supply below demand, distribution selective, and never sell at a discount [196][197]; Richemont bought back €481m of unsold watches in 2016-2018 rather than see them discounted [198]. Decision: a gift page with wishlists, engraving, extended returns to mid-January and the house box; no "sale", no "Black Friday" tag (retired in 09).

### 4.7 The launch model, three examples

1. **Tiffany, the Landmark, 27 April 2023.** A private evening at the temporary store next door: a DJ, the Rockettes, Katy Perry, Mark Ronson; house ambassadors and the Arnault family; Gal Gadot cut the ribbon; the store opened to the public the next day [172][173].
2. **Cartier, Trinity 100, 2024.** A campaign film with five ambassadors; three parties in Paris (Petit Palais, 7 February), New York and London for the three brothers' cities; pop-up exhibitions with free timed entry (Singapore, 15-23 July; Miami Design District, 4-8 December) that ended with a limited collection shown before its global launch [174][175][176].
3. **Van Cleef & Arpels, 2024-25 openings.** Madison Avenue (21 November 2024) with a top-floor private events space; Orange County reopened with a cocktail, a string quartet and a private dinner; Florence (7 May 2025), Zurich and Frankfurt each opened with an exhibition that ran for months [169][170][171].

Our scale (decision): a letter to the review list; a press preview the week before for Nashville Lifestyles, StyleBlueprint and Nfocus; a private evening at the Brentwood bench with the brother setting a stone in front of forty guests; a three-minute film shot at the bench; a printed book of the designs on the Oscar Heyman "Holiday Book" model [204]; the same evening in Mt. Juliet four weeks later.

## 5. Measurement for a luxury local business

### 5.1 Numbers that matter

| Metric | Why | Benchmark or caution, with source |
|---|---|---|
| Appointments booked (site plus GBP) | The site's job is the visit | GBP "bookings" only appear with a supported booking partner [219] |
| Walk-ins attributed | Half the business never books | Ask at the counter; GBP directions requests are de-duplicated and filtered since 2023, so they undercount [219][220] |
| GBP calls, directions, website clicks, search terms | The only free intent data | Calls count button taps only; desktop copy-and-dial is missed, so put a tracking number on the profile [219][220] |
| Review velocity | Recency and rate matter beyond the total | New reviews per 30/60/90 days, days since last, against the top three competitors; vendors' "2-3 a week" targets are unsourced [221] |
| Repair-to-purchase conversion | The repair counter is the acquisition channel | Measure it ourselves; Stuller's 9-in-10 close and 80% return claims are a vendor's [217]; JA found shop services 6-25% of jewelers' business [216] |
| Average ticket | The one number that says "house" | Edge 2024: average ticket +7%, units -2%, gross sales +4% [205]; Edge pool of about 1,000 stores: average retail sale $288, margin flat at 46 for 36 months (undated) [206]; 2025: October sales +13% on units -2% [207]; December average retail price +12% on gold and tariffs [208]; August average retail sale +14% [209] |
| Gross margin | Whether no-discounting holds | JA Cost of Doing Business: 48-49% through the 2000s, median 48.6% in 2008 [214][215] |
| Email reply rate | A letter should get answers | Opens are inflated; count replies and appointments per send. Houses send about one a week [190] |

INSTORE's 2025 Big Survey (630 jewelers, 43 states, fielded September 2025): average product ticket for 2024 clustered at $500-$749 (15%), $750-$999 (14%) and $1,000-$1,499 (13%), with 11% not tracking it; 58% of stores reported sales over $1m; 61% price repairs in line with the Geller book [210][211][212][213]. A house in Brentwood should sit above the modal band and know it to the dollar.

### 5.2 Numbers that mislead

Sessions, bounce rate, time on page and cart abandonment measure a catalog we retired (09 §1). ROAS measures a campaign a house does not run. Follower count is a vanity figure Luxury Society itself dropped in favor of engagement and growth [186]. "Conversion rate" on a site whose job is the appointment should be read as appointments per hundred visits and nothing else.

## 6. Positioning: three candidate sentences

Rules applied: no "first American house" (Black, Starr & Frost and Tiffany hold "first" [3][39]); no claim of making by hand (04 §1.10; the house canon bans the words outright); no origin claim of any kind, the canon's provenance line is the only sentence allowed ("Designed in Tennessee. Set and finished in our workshop. Diamonds and gold are imported."); the lines are never counted.

**A. "An American house of fine jewelry in Tennessee: one designer, one bench, two stores."**
Proof: in-house design and repair on the floor [151]; 2,537 reviews across two stores (09); the lineage in §1 that defines a house by designs, workshop and a named person. Risk: "house" is earned by the signature and the name; without the brother named and one form repeated, it reads as a slogan. Use on the House page.

**B. "The jeweler in Middle Tennessee whose pieces are designed and set in front of you."**
Proof: the open studio the old site already sells ("Gone are the days of someone taking your precious jewelry behind some door", 09 §1); Tacori's glass window and Todd Reed's glass walls as the national precedent [56][71]. Risk: the FTC reads "made" literally; the sentence must sit next to the canon's provenance line ("Designed in Tennessee. Set and finished in our workshop. Diamonds and gold are imported.") and say "set" rather than "made" if casting is outsourced (04 §1.10, §7 question 3). Use on Home.

**C. "Designs you will not find in Green Hills, from a family that has set Tennessee's stones for [N] years."**
Proof: the multi-brand set in §3.3 sells other people's designs; nobody local names a designer of its own pieces. Risk: the site states no founding year [151], so [N] is unknown; the comparative clause invites King and Diamond Cellar to answer. Do not use until the year is confirmed, and even then drop "Green Hills".

Recommendation: A on the House page, B on Home, C never in its comparative form.

## 7. Questions for the family

1. Founding year and founder, exactly as you want it printed; the site states none [151].
2. The brother's full name; will he be named on every design page and photographed at the bench; will he sign the certificate.
3. Casting: in-house or outsourced; stone sourcing; gold sourcing. (Decides sentence B and any origin claim, 04 §2.)
4. Which piece was first, which sells most, which he considers his; is there a Tennessee stone or material story.
5. Trademarks: file the motif names with the house name, or coexist with Lynggaard, Boodles, Jorge and Kinley (§1.3).
6. Pre-owned Rolex: how many a year, and are you comfortable with the exact disclaimer in §3.3.
7. Google review count per store, with the date, behind the 2,537 figure.
8. Any press that has covered the stores before (Nashville Scene, Nashville Lifestyles, StyleBlueprint, the Tennessean).
9. Will the brother set a stone in front of forty guests at a launch evening.
