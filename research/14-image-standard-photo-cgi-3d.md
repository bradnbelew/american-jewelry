# 14. The image standard: photography, CGI and the 3D ring

**For:** American Jewelry remake (Hydrogen on Oxygen; R3F + drei + GSAP + Lenis; Libre Caslon + Public Sans on paper).
**Last updated:** 2026-10-11.
**Why this file exists:** every teardown in 01, 05, 06, 08 and 11 reaches the same conclusion: photography is what reads as luxury, and the site's weakest asset today is stand-in photos. This file turns that into a standard the family, a hired photographer and Claude Code can all follow, and it settles the 3D verdicts that were still open (Theatre.js, WebGPU, how to author "the stone is set").
**Builds on:** 08 §4 (signature feature table), 08 §6 (type, color, photography, motion), 10 §3 (HDRIs, textures, photography references), 11 §5 (photography standard of the houses), 11 §7 (how the best WebGL sites frame 3D). Nothing there is repeated except where a decision changes.
**Method:** WebSearch on 2026-10-11 for the facts cited; forum-level sources are named as such. Anything not confirmed is marked **[verify]**.

## 0. Decisions on one screen

1. **Two image systems, not one.** Computer renders (CGI) for every product-page packshot, because the catalog is Version × Metal and a render family is the only way every variant looks like it was shot on the same day. Photographs for everything with a person, a hand, a bench, a store, and for one macro hero per design. Houses already work this way: the configurator images at the watch and jewelry houses are renders; the campaign images are photographs. (§1)
2. **Renders come from the CAD the brother already makes.** MatrixGold → Blender (Cycles) is free and good enough once the lighting is right; KeyShot is the paid shortcut. One lighting rig file, one camera set, reused for every piece. (§3)
3. **A fixed shot list per piece type**, same camera height, distance and angle set for photos and renders, so the two systems sit side by side without a seam. (§5)
4. **Grounds:** pure white only behind product packshots (also what Google Shopping wants); paper `#F5F2EC` for editorial stills; never a gradient vignette, never a reflection pool. (§2, §3)
5. **Deliver at 2400 px on the long side minimum, sRGB, lossless master kept.** Google's own floor becomes 500 × 500 on 2027-01-31 and its recommendation is 1500 × 1500 or above; ours is higher so a 2× retina 1200 px slot stays sharp. (§1.3)
6. **The web ring stays WebGL.** React Three Fiber + drei `MeshRefractionMaterial`, versions pinned. WebGPU/TSL is not ready for this material and Theatre.js is stalled; neither is adopted. (§4.1, §4.2)
7. **Author "the stone is set" in Blender, not in code.** Animate the band, head, stone and four claws as one sampled glTF clip; on the web, scroll progress sets `action.time`. A designer can re-time the assembly without a deploy. Hand-coded GSAP tweens stay only for the light sweep and the camera. (§4.3)
8. **Honesty rule for renders:** a render on a product page must depict the piece as made (metal color, stone count, proportions). Ready-to-ship pieces get a photograph in the gallery alongside the renders. No render is ever presented as a photograph of a specific one-of-one. (§1.4)
9. **Hire one photographer for two days, not a studio for a season.** The brief in §6 is what to send. (§6)
10. **Nothing from stock, nothing generated by an image model.** HANDOFF rule; restated here because renders are the only "synthetic" images allowed, and only of our own CAD.

## 1. Photograph or render: who does what

### 1.1 What each is good at
- Photography captures what a camera sees: facets, inclusions, the way a prong actually meets a girdle. Buyers scrutinizing a stone want that fidelity. A camera is built to record detail and accuracy; a render simulates it ([Picup Media](https://picupmedia.com/?p=12492), a jewelry-photography vendor, so read it as a partisan but honest statement).
- Rendering shows variants without new shoots: different metals, stones and finishes from one file, and lets the designer check a finish before anything is cast ([XO3D](https://xo3d.co.uk/resources/3d-rendering/jewellery-rendering/), a rendering vendor). Will Gibbons' long comparison is the most balanced read: rendering wins on variants, iteration and impossible camera positions; photography wins on trust for a single high-value object ([willgibbons.com](https://www.willgibbons.com/articles/rendering-vs-photography)).
- No controlled study compares conversion or returns between the two for jewelry; every source is a vendor or a forum. Test both on one piece before scaling.

### 1.2 The split for this house
| Subject | System | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Product-page packshots (3/4, profile, top, macro of the setting) for every Version × Metal | **Render** | Identical light, angle and scale across 60+ variants; new metal = new material, not new shoot |
| One macro hero per design (the Shooting Star tail, the Prism edge) | **Photograph** | The design page's first screen must be unarguably real |
| Hands, wrists, necks | **Photograph** (family hands, daylight) | Rule: no model faces who are not the family; renders of skin are a tell |
| Bench, laser, loupe, the 12-second loop | **Photograph / film** | The most differentiating true fact; must be real |
| Stores, counters, street | **Photograph** | Local pages live or die on this |
| Royal American one-of-ones | **Photograph** (plus the registry entry) | A one-of-one that exists is photographed; a render would be a promise |
| Builder posters, PDP "3D" poster frames | **Render**, same camera as the live R3F view | So the poster-to-canvas cross-fade (11 §7) has no jump |

### 1.3 Delivery specification (both systems)
- Master: 16-bit TIFF or PNG, sRGB IEC61966-2.1, 4000 px long side. Web derivative: 2400 px long side, sRGB, uploaded to Shopify Files; the CDN makes the sizes.
- Packshot frame: square 1:1 at 2400 × 2400, product occupying 75–90 % of the frame, centered, white `#FFFFFF` ground with no vignette, no shadow pool, no border or text. Google Merchant's current minimum is 100 × 100 (apparel 250 × 250), rising to **500 × 500 for all products on 2027-01-31**, with 1500 × 1500 or above recommended; no promotional text, watermarks, borders or padding ([Google Merchant Center: image_link](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324350?hl=en); the 75–90 % fill figure is a third-party rule of thumb from [feedoptimise](https://www.feedoptimise.com/blog/google-shopping-minimum-image-requirements-500x500), not Google policy). Lifestyle images, if ever submitted, need 600 × 600 and an aspect ratio between 2:0 and 2:3 ([lifestyle_image_link](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/9103186)).
- Editorial frame: 4:5 at 2400 × 3000 for phone-first heroes; 3:2 at 3000 × 2000 for desktop bleeds. Art-direct the crop per breakpoint; never letterbox.
- File naming: `{handle}--{metal}--{view}.{ext}` with views `34`, `profile`, `top`, `macro`, `hand`, `hallmark`, `poster`. The site already keys photos by name in `app/lib/catalog/photos.ts`; keep that contract.
- Color: shoot and render against a gray card / known HDRI; export sRGB; never let yellow gold drift orange or white gold drift blue. Check skin in hand shots against a neutral.

### 1.4 Honesty and law
- The FTC Jewelry Guides do not forbid renders, but any image that misrepresents the product is a deceptive practice under Section 5 (research/04 Part 1). Practical rules: a render must match the cast piece's stone count, metal color and proportion; a render of a size-7 ring must not be captioned as a one-of-one that exists; never composite a stone larger than the set quality.
- Disclosure: the industry does not caption renders and the law does not require it. We do two things anyway: ready-to-ship pieces show at least one photograph, and the House page says plainly that product images are renders of the house's own drawings and that pieces are photographed when they exist. That sentence is a trust asset, not a confession.

## 2. The photography standard

### 2.1 The one book
*Light: Science and Magic* (Hunter, Biver, Fuqua; Routledge) is the reference for photographing metal and glass. Its method is the **family of angles**: a polished surface is a mirror, so it shows the camera whatever sits at the mirror angle; you light by deciding what the metal will reflect (a large white or paper surface) and where the black cards go, instead of pointing lights at the piece. The table of contents lists "The Management of Reflection and the Family of Angles" and, in the metal chapter, "Finding the family of angles" ([O'Reilly listing](https://oreilly.com/library/view/light-science-and/9780240808192)). Buy the current edition for the family and the photographer; it is the single most useful thing in this file.

### 2.2 Set-up that the houses' packshots imply (06, 11 §5)
- **Light tent or cone, not softboxes.** Rings and settings have surfaces at every angle, so the reflected environment must be continuous. A translucent cone over the piece, lit from outside, gives graduated, controlled reflections and kills the room ([Visual Education ring class](https://visualeducation.com/class/jewellery-photography-rings/), [Adaptalux macro guide](https://adaptalux.com/jewellery-macro-photography/)). Black cards at the edge of the family of angles give the metal its dark edge and the stones their contrast; without them polished gold photographs as flat yellow.
- **Macro lens 90–105 mm, f/8–f/11, camera locked on a rail.** Focus stacking is mandatory for anything with a stone: lock the camera, rack focus from the table of the stone to the back of the shank in 8–20 frames, blend in Helicon Focus or Zerene ([Packshot Creator](https://www.packshot-creator.com/how-to/photograph-rings-white), [Gemfind guide](https://gemfind.com/blogs/web-design/jewelry-photography-complete-guide-2026)). Without a stack, either the prongs or the stone is soft, and soft prongs read as cheap.
- **Clean the piece under magnification before every frame.** Dust is the most common retouch cost.
- **Ground:** white acrylic for packshots (retouched to pure white), paper stock in the house paper tone for editorial stills. No "infinity" gradient, no mirror floor (a Jared's tell per 08 §1).
- **Hands:** daylight from a window, north light if possible; the family's hands; short, clean nails without color; one ring per hand unless the design is a stack; wrist and neck shots at the same scale rules. No faces unless they are the family's.
- **Scale:** a hand of known size is the honest scale reference; the site does not promise true scale (family rule), so the photograph does that job.

### 2.3 Retouch rules
Remove dust, fingerprints and the photographer's reflection. Never enlarge a stone, never add fire, never paint out a true inclusion on a one-of-one (it is the stone's identity and is on the report). Keep the hallmark legible. A customer who holds the piece should recognize the photograph.

## 3. The CGI standard

### 3.1 Pipeline
1. **Source:** the brother's MatrixGold files (Rhino-based). Export per piece: the full model as 3DM/STL at production scale, with stones as separate objects named by seat, so materials can be assigned by name.
2. **Renderer:** Blender 4.x Cycles (free). KeyShot is the paid alternative most jewelers use because it is "probably the most user-friendly" ([McNeel forum](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/rendering-software-for-jewellery-design/49884?page=3)); MatrixGold's built-in render is reported as hard to light for stones ([McNeel forum](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/matrixgold/187799), anecdotal). Start in Blender; move to KeyShot only if the first ten renders eat more than a day each.
3. **One rig file.** A single `.blend` holding: the studio HDRI, two rectangular area lights as softboxes with black flags, the camera set (§5), the gold/platinum/stone materials, and the white and paper grounds. Every piece is linked into this file; nothing is re-lit per piece. This is what makes sixty variants look like one family.
4. **Environment:** Poly Haven studio HDRIs are CC0 and already short-listed in research/10 §3 (`brown_photostudio_02`, `cyclorama_hard_light`). The HDRI matters more than the shader: a flat, white, sparkle-less stone is usually the wrong environment, and many large soft sources wash out a stone's dark areas and kill its contrast ([iJewel diamond materials note](https://docs.ijewel3d.com/playground/diamond-materials.html); [Blender Artists caustics thread](https://blenderartists.org/t/how-to-achieve-realistic-diamond-caustics-and-light-play-in-cycles/1605044)). Use a high-contrast environment with a few small bright sources.
5. **Stone material:** Cycles does not do spectral dispersion natively. The standard trick is three glass shaders at per-channel IORs combined with an Add shader; a documented set for diamond is R 2.410, G 2.426, B 2.444 ([Blender Artists, Material Study: Diamond v2](https://blenderartists.org/t/material-study-diamond-v2/635369); the derivation of per-channel IOR from published dispersion data is argued at [Blendkit](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/blendkit-dispersion-materials), a product page). True caustics need LuxCore or Octane; we do not need caustics for packshots. Check the Blender manual for your version before relying on the three-shader trick; newer releases may expose a dispersion input **[verify]**.
6. **Metal materials:** Principled BSDF, metallic 1.0. Polished roughness 0.12–0.20; satin 0.35–0.45. Base colors are the one place to be careful: pure gold's measured linear reflectance is about (1.00, 0.77, 0.34); 18k yellow is slightly paler and 14k paler still; rose gold shifts toward copper; rhodium-plated white gold and platinum are near-neutral grays (platinum slightly warmer and darker than rhodium). Treat the exact numbers as starting points to be matched against a photographed reference piece under the same HDRI ([physicallybased.info](https://physicallybased.info/) lists measured metal values **[verify exact figures]**). The 2012 BlendSwap gold presets are dated but show the roughness logic ([BlendSwap 4672](https://blendswap.com/blend/4672)).
7. **Geometry:** add the fillets the real piece has. "Poorly prepared" jewelry with sharp CAD edges and perfectly flat surfaces is the most common reason a render looks like a render ([McNeel forum](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/rendering-software-for-jewellery-design/49884?page=6)). A 0.1 mm bevel on every edge and a 0.5 % surface noise on polished planes make the difference.
8. **Output:** 2400 px square packshots, 16-bit, denoised, with a separate alpha so the white ground can be composited exactly; film-grain is never added to renders.

### 3.2 Reference match
Before producing the catalog, render one piece that exists (a Signal band in yellow) and photograph the same piece under §2. Adjust HDRI exposure and metal base color until the two sit side by side without a seam at 100 %. Keep that pair as the calibration standard in `research/shots/calibration/` (gitignored like the other shots).

### 3.3 Cost of ownership
Blender renders of a ring with a refracted stone take minutes per frame on a modern GPU; a 2012 forum report of 30 minutes on a GTX 750 is a floor, not a guide ([BlenderNation](https://www.blendernation.com/2012/04/05/cycles-diamond-dispersion/)). Budget one evening per design for the first family and under an hour per variant afterward. The rig file is the investment.

## 4. The web ring: verdicts and the authoring path

### 4.1 Renderer verdict: WebGL, pinned
- drei's `MeshRefractionMaterial` (ray-cast bounces, IOR, `aberrationStrength` for dispersion, `fastChroma`) is the right stone material and the one already in `ParametricRing` ([drei docs](https://drei.docs.pmnd.rs/shaders/mesh-refraction-material)). It is a WebGL shader; its docs do not mention WebGPU.
- drei's shader materials have broken under the WebGPU renderer: `MeshTransmissionMaterial` stopped working at three 0.180 and the thread asks for a TSL port with no confirmed fix ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/drei-meshtransmissionmaterial-webgpu/87427)). Codrops' 2026 WebGPU work writes refraction in TSL by hand ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/?p=120372)). So: stay on the WebGL renderer, pin `three`, `@react-three/fiber` and `@react-three/drei` exactly, and test the stone after every bump.
- Fallback if the material ever breaks: `MeshPhysicalMaterial` now has a `dispersion` property; it shows less of the stone's internal facets but is maintained by three.js core ([same forum thread](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/drei-meshtransmissionmaterial-webgpu/87427)).

### 4.2 Sequencer verdict: no Theatre.js
`@theatre/r3f` is at 0.7.2, its README still says the API "can and will drastically change," its last release notes are from 2023-08-10 and the extension's source directory last changed in 2023-09 ([npm](https://npmjs.com/package/@theatre/r3f), [release notes](https://theatrejs.com/docs/0.5/releases), [source mirror](https://git.pointer.click/variablelab/theatre/src/branch/main/packages/r3f)). It requires three ≥ 0.155 and R3F ≥ 8.13; we are on R3F 9. Do not adopt.

### 4.3 The authoring path for "the stone is set"
The feature is specified in 08 §4 and framed in 11 §7. What was open is **where the keyframes live**. Decision: in Blender.

1. In the rig file, animate the assembly over 100 frames: band arrives (0–30), head rises (20–55), stone lowers (50–80), four claws close (75–95), rest (95–100). Keep every object a separate mesh with its own transform; the claws are four objects, not one.
2. Export GLB with **sampled animation** on so constraints bake into plain keyframes ([Blender glTF exporter commit](https://lists.blender.org/pipermail/bf-extensions-cvs/2019-May/008163.html)), and trim static channels so the file stays small ([three.js forum on static keyframes](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/filtering-out-static-keyframes-exporting-animation-data-to-fbx-file/5997)). Run `gltf-transform` (already a dev dependency) for Draco.
3. On the web: one `AnimationMixer` on the ring root; `clipAction(clip).play()` then `paused = true`; GSAP ScrollTrigger with `scrub: 1` tweens a proxy `{ t: 0 → 1 }` and `useFrame` sets `action.time = t * clip.duration` and calls `mixer.update(0)` ([three.js forum pattern](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/how-to-control-gltf-animation-while-scrolling-the-page-using-three-js-gsap/52026), [GSAP + mixer thread](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/gsap-animationmixer/34777)). If several clips are exported, one mixer per animated object avoids the "only the last object scrubs" bug ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/animating-blender-objects-on-scroll/37981)).
4. Damp fast scrolling: clamp the per-frame change of `t` (maath `damp` or a max delta) so a flick does not jump the stone ([same forum thread](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/how-to-control-gltf-animation-while-scrolling-the-page-using-three-js-gsap/52026)). Test portrait phones specifically; one thread reports a scrub that only ran after rotating the device.
5. The light sweep and the camera stay in code (GSAP on the Lightformer intensity and the camera preset), because they depend on viewport size.
6. Poster, reduced motion, one-viewport pin, no idle spin: unchanged from 11 §7.

Why this beats hand-coded tweens: the brother can re-time the assembly in Blender and hand over a new GLB; the designer, not the engineer, owns how the stone is set. It also gives the image-sequence fallback for free.

### 4.4 The image-sequence fallback
Render the same 100 frames from the rig at 1200 × 1500 as WebP (about 25–40 KB each at quality 80), and scrub them on a `<canvas>` for devices that fail the WebGL check or report `prefers-reduced-data`. Apple's product pages use this pattern (08 §4 row 1). It costs 3–4 MB per design page and should load lazily, after the poster.

### 4.5 Budgets (restated so they are in one place)
GLB under 1.5 MB after Draco; textures KTX2; HDRI 1k; canvas mounted after first paint and never in the first 200 ms of main-thread time; LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1 at p75 (research/10 §4). Deloitte's retail finding that 0.1 s of mobile speed moved conversion 8.4 % is the business reason (cited in the first reply to the owner; [Deloitte, Milliseconds Make Millions](https://www2.deloitte.com/ie/en/pages/consulting/articles/milliseconds-make-millions.html) **[verify URL]**).

## 6. The photographer brief (send this)

**Who:** a product and still-life photographer who has shot reflective objects; ask to see metal and glass in their book, not food. Nashville has editorial still-life photographers; a jewelry specialist is not required if they own a cone or tent and stack focus.

**Days:** two. Day one, packshots and macros for the calibration pair and the Royal American pieces that exist, plus the hallmark frames. Day two, hands and people at both stores in daylight, the bench film loop (locked-off camera, 12 s, no music, natural sound), store exteriors and counters at opening.

**Deliverables:** masters per §1.3, color-managed, focus-stacked where stated, retouched per §2.3, with a contact sheet. Film: 4K ProRes master plus a 1080p web loop.

**Rights:** full buy-out for web, print, social and advertising in perpetuity; credit optional; raw files delivered. Model releases for every family member photographed. No stock, no AI generation or AI enhancement anywhere in the delivery; state it in the contract.

**What we bring:** cleaned pieces, the paper stock, the gray card, the shot list above printed, the calibration piece.
