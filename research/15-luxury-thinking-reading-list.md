# 15. How to think like a house: the reading list and the practice

**For:** the owner, the designer and anyone briefing Claude Code on American Jewelry.
**Last updated:** 2026-10-11.
**What this is:** research/10 §4 covers web craft (Rauno, Emil Kowalski, Linear, Vercel, Utopia, Capsize, Baymard). This file covers everything upstream of the screen: why luxury breaks ordinary marketing, what status signalling actually is, how the best houses write, what the design classics teach about emptiness and type, how to photograph metal, and which American jewelry books carry the lineage this house wants to join. Each entry says what it is, why it matters to this brand, and how to use it. Verified items carry a URL; books are cited by author and publisher; anything uncertain is marked **[verify]**.
**Rule for reading:** one chapter, then one change on the site. Reading without applying is how taste stays theoretical.

---

## 0. The ten that matter most, in order

1. Kapferer & Bastien, *The Luxury Strategy* (Kogan Page). The anti-laws. Read first.
2. Tuch et al. 2012 (Google / Basel). Low visual complexity plus high prototypicality wins in 17–50 ms. Read second, because it settles "unique vs familiar."
3. Han, Nunes & Drèze 2010, *Journal of Marketing*. Quiet goods cost more and are bought by the people we want.
4. Baymard, luxury e-commerce benchmark (2021, 2024). Six of seven luxury sites are mediocre at usability; function is the opening.
5. Butterick, *Practical Typography* (free). Then Bringhurst.
6. Hunter, Biver & Fuqua, *Light: Science and Magic*. The family of angles.
7. Kenya Hara, *White* (Lars Müller). Emptiness as a container for meaning; the design philosophy behind paper and one subject per screen.
8. Verlyn Klinkenborg, *Several Short Sentences About Writing* (Vintage). The house voice in a book.
9. Peltason, *David Webb: The Quintessential American Jeweler* (Rizzoli). The American house that still makes everything above its store.
10. Christie's and Sotheby's jewelry catalogues (free online). Copywork material.

## 1. Luxury strategy: why the ordinary rules invert

- **Kapferer & Bastien, *The Luxury Strategy: Break the Rules of Marketing to Build Luxury Brands*, Kogan Page, 3rd ed.** The chapter of "anti-laws of marketing" is the canon: the number varies by edition (18 in one summary, 24 in another), and they reject target marketing, positioning, market research, celebrity endorsement, promotion, volume growth and selling online as default behaviors ([Iowa State ITAA summary](https://www.iastatedigitalpress.com/itaa/article/id/3346/), [Kogan Page](https://www.koganpage.com/product/the-luxury-strategy-9781398624269)). Specific anti-laws documented in that summary: do not respond to rising demand; many more people should know and dream about the brand than can buy it; do not pander to your customer's wishes (the BMW rear-seat example). *Why for us:* the family's rules (no discounts, no urgency, no counting the lines, price on request for Royal American, viewing by appointment) are these anti-laws arrived at by instinct. Reading the book turns instinct into a defensible plan. *Use:* list each anti-law, write one line on how the site obeys or breaks it.
- **Chevalier & Mazzalovo, *Luxury Brand Management: A World of Privilege*, Wiley.** The operational companion: distribution, the store as temple, the role of the designer. *Use:* the chapters on retail and on brand identity.
- **Dana Thomas, *Deluxe: How Luxury Lost Its Luster*, Penguin.** What happens when houses chase volume. *Why:* the cautionary tale for a house that wants to grow; it explains why "jareds.com" is the enemy.
- **Bain & Company with Altagamma, the annual Luxury Study.** Free summaries each November; the numbers on who buys, where, and how the top tier behaves ([bain.com](https://www.bain.com/insights/topics/luxury-goods-worldwide-market-study/) **[verify current URL]**).
- **Dion & Arnould (2011), "Retail Luxury Strategy: Assembling Charisma through Art and Magic," *Journal of Retailing* 87(4).** How luxury stores manufacture aura: the creative director as artist, the store as a stage, objects shown as art. *Why:* the House page and the stores are where this happens for us; the bench you can watch is our "magic." **[verify citation]**

## 2. Status, signalling and why quiet costs more

- **Han, Nunes & Drèze (2010), "Signaling Status with Luxury Goods: The Role of Brand Prominence," *Journal of Marketing* 74(4), 15–30, DOI 10.1509/jmkg.74.4.15.** Brand prominence (how loud the logo is) sorts buyers into Patricians (quiet, wealthy, low status need), Parvenus (loud, wealthy), Poseurs (loud counterfeits) and Proletarians; luxury brands charge more for quieter items ([AMA page](https://www.ama.org/2021/04/16/signaling-status-with-luxury-goods-the-role-of-brand-prominence/), [PDF via gwern](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/culture/2010-han.pdf)). *Why:* the hallmark struck inside the shank, legible only to the wearer, is exactly the Patrician signal. The logo being paused is not a problem; prominence is the wrong lever anyway.
- **Pracejus, Olsen & O'Guinn (2006), "How Nothing Became Something: White Space, Rhetoric, History, and Meaning," *Journal of Consumer Research* 33(1), 82–90, DOI 10.1086/504138.** White space means prestige, and the meaning is shared by the people who make ads and the people who read them ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v33y2006i1p82-90.html)). *Why:* evidence for `--section-y: 12vh` and one subject per screen when someone asks "why so empty."
- **Thorstein Veblen, *The Theory of the Leisure Class* (1899).** Public domain. Conspicuous consumption, conspicuous leisure, and the origin of the word "Veblen good." *Use:* chapter IV only.
- **Rory Sutherland, *Alchemy* (William Morrow).** Costly signalling in plain English: why effort and expense in the wrong places (a letter, a slow process, a registry) are the message. *Why:* it makes the case for the four-to-six-week lead time as a feature.
- **Will Storr, *The Status Game* (William Collins).** Why people buy symbols; the three games (dominance, virtue, success). *Use:* the chapter on prestige games for how a house earns deference instead of attention.
- **Wadhwa & Zhang (2015), "This Number Just Feels Right: The Impact of Roundedness of Price Numbers on Product Evaluations," *Journal of Consumer Research* 41(5).** Round prices suit purchases made on feeling ([JCR via OUP](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/41/5/1172/2962104) **[verify URL]**). *Why:* "$4,800" not "$4,795" is the house's price grammar, and here is the evidence.

## 3. First impressions and usability evidence (the short list; details in 10 §4 and the first reply)

- **Tuch, Presslaber, Stöcklin, Opwis & Bargas-Avila (2012), "The role of visual complexity and prototypicality regarding first impression of websites," *Int. J. Human-Computer Studies* 70(11).** Judged in 17–50 ms; low complexity and high prototypicality rated most appealing; prototypicality matters more the longer people look ([Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/pub38315/), [Google blog](https://ai.googleblog.com/2012/08/users-love-simple-and-familiar-designs.html)). *Rule:* spend the uniqueness in photography, type, copy and one motion moment; keep navigation, product page and bag where people expect them.
- **Lindgaard, Fernandes, Dudek & Brown (2006), "Attention web designers: You have 50 milliseconds to make a good first impression!" *Behaviour & Information Technology* 25(2).** The 50 ms figure everyone quotes **[verify citation]**.
- **Baymard Institute**, luxury e-commerce benchmark (2021 free article; 2024 summary) and the Jewelry & Watches research area ([2021](https://baymard.com/research-articles/2021-luxury-ecommerce-benchmark), [jewelry](https://baymard.com/research/jewelry-and-watches)). Jakob Nielsen's reading: six of seven luxury sites "mediocre," one decent ([Nielsen roundup](https://jakobnielsenphd.substack.com/p/ux-roundup-20240429?open=false)).
- **NN/g, Kate Moran:** "Luxury Shopping User Groups and Journeys" and "Why So Many Luxury Brands Are Terrible at Ecommerce" ([author page](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/author/kate-moran/?page=7)). Four shopper types; failure in the consider phase.

## 4. Writing: the house voice

- **Verlyn Klinkenborg, *Several Short Sentences About Writing*, Vintage.** Short declarative sentences, one idea each, no connective tissue. This is the Rolex register taught as a method. *Use:* read it, then rewrite the House letter.
- **William Zinsser, *On Writing Well*, Harper.** Clutter and the first-person honesty of the best nonfiction. *Use:* the chapters "Simplicity," "Clutter," "Style."
- **Strunk & White, *The Elements of Style*.** Omit needless words. Public-domain first edition online.
- **David Ogilvy, *Ogilvy on Advertising*, Vintage.** Long copy sells when the reader is interested; the Rolls-Royce clock headline is specificity as luxury. *Why:* the product-page sentence and the journal should be specific, not short for its own sake.
- **Luke Sullivan, *Hey Whipple, Squeeze This*, Wiley.** Concepting: how to find the one true thing about a product and say only that.
- **Auction catalogues as copywork.** Christie's and Sotheby's lot pages: a title line of maker, material and date; a terse spec block; a provenance section listing prior sales by sale number and lot; a separate condition report; "French assay mark for platinum" as a sentence. Read ten lots from a Magnificent Jewels sale ([Sotheby's example](https://www.sothebys.com/en/auctions/ecatalogue/2012/magnificent-jewels-n08843/lot.365.html), [Christie's example](https://onlineonly.christies.com.cn/s/splendor-style-jewels-collection-distinguished-lady/van-cleef-arpels-retro-ruby-diamond-clip-brooch-441/285132)). Sotheby's also states that stones are assumed treated unless the catalogue says natural; that candor is a model for our stone lines. *Use:* copy twenty lot descriptions by hand, then write the Royal American registry entries in that grammar.
- **House copy to study (05, 06):** Rolex spec lines; Patek's "You never actually own a Patek Philippe"; Hermès product descriptions (material, dimensions, one sentence of use). Copy twenty of each.

## 5. Design classics: emptiness, type, grid, color

- **Kenya Hara, *White* and *Designing Design*, Lars Müller.** Emptiness as potential; the paper tradition; why a page that holds back feels expensive. The philosophical ground of "one subject per screen."
- **Jun'ichirō Tanizaki, *In Praise of Shadows*, Leete's Island Books.** Luxury as the quality of shadow, patina and dimness. *Why:* it argues against glare, which is the whole problem with how most jewelry is lit and shown.
- **Dieter Rams, ten principles of good design** (Vitsœ publishes them free: [vitsoe.com](https://www.vitsoe.com/us/about/good-design)). "As little design as possible."
- **Massimo Vignelli, *The Vignelli Canon*** (free PDF from vignelli.com **[verify link]**). Semantics, syntactics, pragmatics; the discipline of a few typefaces and a grid.
- **Robert Bringhurst, *The Elements of Typographic Style*, Hartley & Marks.** The canon; the frontend-design skill already asks for its scale guidance.
- **Matthew Butterick, *Practical Typography*** (free: [practicaltypography.com](https://practicaltypography.com/)). The fastest route from zero to competent. Read "Typography in ten minutes" and the chapters on point size, line length, line spacing.
- **Jost Hochuli, *Detail in Typography*, Hyphen Press.** Letterspacing, word spacing, the micro-decisions that make Caslon look like Caslon.
- **Ellen Lupton, *Thinking with Type*, Princeton Architectural Press.** The approachable one; good for the family.
- **Josef Müller-Brockmann, *Grid Systems in Graphic Design*, Niggli.** Asymmetric grids, the 7/5 split we use.
- **Armin Hofmann, *Graphic Design Manual*, Niggli.** Form, contrast, the dot and the line.
- **Josef Albers, *Interaction of Color*, Yale.** Why gold reads differently on paper and on navy; why we keep gold at 24 px and above.
- **Jan Tschichold, *The Form of the Book*, Hartley & Marks.** Margins and proportion; the page as an object.
- **Edward Tufte, *Visual Explanations* and *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, Graphics Press.** For the registry table, the stone report and the price ledger: tables as objects of respect.
- **Adam Wathan & Steve Schoger, *Refactoring UI*.** Practical hierarchy and spacing; read after the classics so it does not become the style.
- **Heydon Pickering & Andy Bell, *Every Layout*** ([every-layout.dev](https://every-layout.dev/)). Algorithmic layout primitives; the Stack, the Sidebar, the Cover. The right way to build our few layouts in CSS.

## 6. The type story we already own

- The Dunlap broadside of the Declaration of Independence (July 4–5, 1776) was set in **Caslon**, English casting, body in the size then called English (about 14 pt) ([Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/1666/the-dunlap-broadside), [Penn Libraries](https://www.library.upenn.edu/events/typography-independence/makers-blog/printing-and-type), [Alexander Lawson archive](https://alexanderslawsonarchive.com/july-4/)). Goddard's 1777 broadside also used Caslon.
- **Public Sans** is the U.S. Web Design System's typeface, forked from Libre Franklin, which is Impallari Type's update of Morris Fuller Benton's 1912 Franklin Gothic; SIL OFL 1.1; designed by Dan Williams with Pablo Impallari and Rodrigo Fuenzalida ([AIGA Eye on Design](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/the-u-s-governments-public-sans-is-a-typeface-fit-for-civic-duty/), [18F repo](https://github.com/18F/18franklin/)).
- *Why this matters:* the house's two typefaces are the typeface of the Declaration and the typeface of the American government, both free and both legitimately ours. That is a one-paragraph House-page story that no other jeweler can tell, and it is true. It also answers "why not a custom typeface": the choice is already a statement. If a licensed upgrade is ever wanted, the Caslon family has serious cuts (Adobe Caslon, Big Caslon, Williams Caslon Text, Caslon Doric) **[verify current licensing]**; none is needed for launch.

## 7. Photography and film

- **Hunter, Biver & Fuqua, *Light: Science and Magic*, Routledge.** See research/14 §2.

## 8. The American lineage, in books (the agent's file research/13 covers the houses themselves)

- **Ruth Peltason, *David Webb: The Quintessential American Jeweler*, Rizzoli.** The house that still makes every piece in its own New York workshop; animals, enamel, hammered gold.
- **Patricia Corbett, *Verdura: The Life and Work of a Master Jeweler*, Thames & Hudson.** Fulco di Verdura, New York 1939; the Maltese cross, the wrapped heart.
- **Amanda Vaill & Janet Zapata, *Seaman Schepps: A Century of New York Jewelry Design*, Vendome.** Shells, wood, color; the 1930s New York house.
- **Yvonne Markowitz, *Oscar Heyman: The Jewelers' Jeweler*, MFA Publications (2017).** A family firm since 1912 that made for Cartier, Tiffany and Van Cleef; still family-owned. The closest model for "the bench is the brand."
- **John Loring, *Tiffany Style: 170 Years of Design*, Abrams** (and his other Tiffany volumes). How an American house built a design vocabulary.
- **Dorothy Rainwater, *American Jewelry Manufacturers*, Schiffer.** The reference on American makers' marks. *Why:* when the house registers and strikes its own mark, this is the tradition it joins (research/12 covers the law).
- **Penny Proddow & Debra Healy, *American Jewelry: Glamour and Tradition*, Rizzoli.** The survey of American houses from Tiffany to Webb **[verify in print]**.

- **GIA**: free articles and the *Gems & Gemology* journal archive ([gia.edu](https://www.gia.edu/gems-gemology)). The 4Cs as GIA defines them, so our spec lines use the grader's words.
- **Jewelers Vigilance Committee**: the plain-English guides to the FTC Jewelry Guides and to origin claims ([jvclegal.org](https://www.jvclegal.org/)). Research/04 and /12 lean on them.
- **JCK, National Jeweler, Rapaport, INSTORE**: trade press; INSTORE's "Big Survey" has independent-jeweler benchmarks (ticket, margin, marketing spend) each year **[verify access]**.
- **The Knot Real Weddings Study** (annual; 52 % man-made centers in the 2025 study of 2024 weddings; a later survey reported 61 %) ([CNBC/NBC](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/more-couples-are-choosing-lab-grown-diamonds-over-natural-stones/6179592/), [Rapaport](https://rapaport.com/news/lab-grown-taking-over-engagement-ring-spending-the-knot/)). New American is the majority bridal product, not the side line.
- **De Beers Diamond Insight Report** and the **Natural Diamond Council**: the natural-stone side of the argument, for the Americana story ([naturaldiamonds.com](https://www.naturaldiamonds.com/)) **[verify]**.

## 10. The practice (what turns reading into a top-1 % site)

1. **Teardown with instruments, not adjectives.** On Cartier, Rolex, Hermès and David Webb: Chrome DevTools → More tools → CSS Overview for colors, fonts and media queries ([Chrome DevTools tips](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/devtools-tips-1/)); the Animations panel for real durations and easing; PageSpeed Insights for field data. Write the numbers down beside ours.
2. **Copywork, by hand.** Twenty auction lots, twenty Rolex spec lines, twenty Hermès product sentences. Then write ours.
3. **One rebuild a week.** Recreate one screen of a reference site to the pixel in a scratch file. Spacing is learned in the hands.
4. **Five-second tests and five-user tests.** Nielsen's 5 users find most problems; a five-second screenshot test on ten strangers tells you what the hero says. The stores' customers are the panel; the 2,537 reviews are the vocabulary.
5. **Critique in numbers.** "Title wraps to 3 lines at 390; make it 2" is a note Claude Code can act on; "feels off" is not. See research/16 for the method and the house critic.
6. **Ship, then watch.** Microsoft Clarity recordings (free) and Shopify analytics after launch; Baymard's checklist for the product page once a quarter.

## 11. A thirty-day order
- Week 1: Kapferer & Bastien anti-laws chapter; Tuch 2012; Butterick's ten minutes. Change: the price grammar and the hero.
- Week 2: Klinkenborg; twenty auction lots; Rolex spec lines. Change: the House letter and every product sentence.
- Week 3: *Light: Science and Magic* chapters on reflection and metal; Hara's *White*. Change: the photographer brief (research/14 §6) and the grounds.
- Week 4: Peltason on Webb; Markowitz on Heyman; Rainwater on marks. Change: the House page lineage paragraph and the hallmark plan (research/12).
