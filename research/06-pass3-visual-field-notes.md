# 06 — Visual field notes from the luxury pass (session 3, 2026-10-09)

Companion to `05-watch-houses-visual-copy-study.md` (text and copy, by the research agent) and `01-luxury-house-teardowns.md` (sitemaps). These notes are what the pages actually **look** like, taken from full-page headless captures at 1440 px and live scrolling in the browser pane. Where a site walled itself off (Ralph Lauren: press-and-hold bot gate; Audemars Piguet and Roberto Coin: scripts refused to render headless) it is marked and not described. Captures live in `research/shots/houses/` (gitignored; competitor material).

## What each site looks like, and how it stands out

**Rolex.** Chrome is almost nothing: a hamburger, the crown centered, three text utilities (Search, Store locator, Favorites). No visible nav words at all. The homepage is a full-viewport video with a tiny uppercase eyebrow ("ROLEX AND MUSIC"), a two-line bold headline and one pill button, then a horizontal rail of cards, then "The Rolex collection" with a "Continue" pill, each watch family as a full-width band: one watch on a soft studio gradient, name, one line, "Discover more". The product page is the same discipline turned vertical: title block at the LEFT of the first viewport ("Day-Date 40 / Oyster, 40 mm, yellow gold / Reference 228238 / $50,400 USD / Add to favorites"), the watch centered on a pale gradient, dial swatches at right ("View variations"), "Discover in 360°" below. Then the page becomes a story: a giant sentence whose material words are highlighted ("…gold with a white dial, fluted bezel and a President bracelet."), then each feature as its own full-screen chapter with one close-up (the fluted bezel alone on grey) and a paragraph, with a dashed scroll-progress column at the right edge. Footer is a plain text sitemap with a dark hero band ("The Oyster Story") before it. **Stands out by rhythm, not decoration: one subject per screen, the same grammar on every card, bold sans type, green used only for buttons.**

**Patek Philippe.** Centered small logo, hamburger "MENU", thin uppercase display type ("LEGENDARY PURITY / CALATRAVA"), grey-washed photography, long alternating image/text blocks with enormous whitespace, a quote block with a signature. Everything is lowercase-energy, hushed; the cookie bar is the loudest thing on the page. **Stands out by restraint and by writing in "we" about family and duty.**

**Van Cleef & Arpels.** Serif wordmark, a dancer figurine as the hero on grey, full-width bands that change background color per story (grey, dark, warm beige, pale blue, wood texture). The product page is a textbook: product alone on pale grey, two on-model photos below it, a navy editorial quote block, then "COMPLETE SET / EXPLORE OTHER CREATIONS" tabs with sibling pieces and prices ("+2 stone variations"). **Stands out by whimsy (dance, luck, color) inside a strict grid.**

**Cartier.** Centered script wordmark, eight text nav items plus utilities, a full-viewport campaign video ("ENDLESS LOVE") with giant typographic overlay. **Stands out by scale of the campaign asset; the chrome is conventional.**

**Harry Winston.** Hamburger + centered wordmark + search only. Full-viewport portrait of a model with the necklace, headline bottom-left ("Majestic Escapes by Harry Winston"), a tiny "DISCOVER" button, a thin gold hairline rule under centered titles. The engagement listing opens with "Please Select Diamond Shape" as a row of seven shape icons, then a grid where one 2x2 lifestyle tile (hand, box, navy ribbon) sits among 1x1 product shots on pale grey with **no text under the tiles**. **Stands out by the gold hairline, the navy ribbon, and faces.**

**Tiffany.** A promo bar on robin's-egg blue, big serif wordmark, split hero (text left, model right), product carousel on white, "Shop by Category" photo tiles, "Icon Collections" as two large photographs. **Stands out by its color and by selling categories as photographs.**

**David Yurman, Roberto Coin, Ralph Lauren, Audemars Piguet.** Not captured visually this pass (walls or script rendering); see research 05 for what their text reveals.

## What we took, and what we deliberately did not

Taken:
- One subject per screen on tier and design pages (Rolex family bands → our design bands with the name set over the photograph).
- A title block with a reference line and a plain tax note on the product page (Rolex).
- A composed one-sentence description with the material words carried in ink (Rolex's highlighted sentence).
- "Complete the set" as the sibling grid (Van Cleef).
- Four-line product cards with no badges (Rolex).
- Centered quiet openers only where the message is the page (Patek's hush, used on appointments, policies, not-found).
- Chrome kept to text; no icons; the wordmark centered (every house).

Not taken, on purpose:
- Full-viewport autoplay video heroes (Rolex, Cartier, HW). The family has no film yet, and a still photograph with a short headline reads calmer at our scale.
- Hamburger-only navigation (Rolex, HW, Patek). A two-store regional house benefits from visible words; we keep eight items on one line.
- Changing background colors per band (Van Cleef). Theme lock: white with one paper tint.
- Faces and models as heroes (HW, Tiffany). Unverifiable for us; the bench, the stones and the place carry the authority instead.
- Scroll-progress rails, pinned chapters and scroll-jacking (Rolex). A CSS-only reveal is the whole motion budget.
- Shape-icon rows and diamond selectors (HW, Tiffany, Graff). The family sells set qualities at set sizes; no stone browsing.

## What still separates us from the bar

1. **Photography.** Theirs is studio product photography on controlled grey or gradient grounds, four fixed views per piece, one close-up per feature. Ours is the family's current store photography standing in for designs that do not exist yet. Nothing on the page will read as "house" until the pieces are shot that way.
2. **Named people.** Yurman, Patek and Winston name the family; our House page is a letter signed "The family" because no names were supplied.
3. **A film.** Every house opens with motion. A ten-second loop of the bench would do more than any layout change.
4. **Real hours, a real year, a real logo.** All three are placeholders.
