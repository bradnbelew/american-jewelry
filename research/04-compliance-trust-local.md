## Part 1 — FTC Jewelry Guides (16 CFR Part 23, 2018 revision)

### 1.1 What the Guides are and how they bite
- The Guides are **administrative interpretations of Section 5 of the FTC Act**, not rules; they tell marketers how the FTC will evaluate jewelry claims. Non-compliance is not itself a violation, but the underlying deceptive practice is, and the FTC enforces with warning letters, consent orders and (where a rule or order is violated) civil penalties. § 23.0 says the Guides apply to "labeling, advertising, promotional materials, and all other forms of marketing," whether the claim is made by "words, symbols, emblems, logos, illustrations, depictions, product brand names," and that deception is judged by "the net impression of the advertisement, label, or other promotional material." Source: 2018 final notice, 83 FR 40665 — https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2018-08-16/html/2018-17454.htm
- § 23.1 (deception, general) covers misrepresenting "type, kind, grade, quality, quantity, metallic content, size, weight, cut, color, character, treatment, substance, durability, serviceability, origin, price, value, preparation, production, manufacture, distribution, or any other material aspect." Note 2: "Qualifications or disclosures should be sufficiently clear and prominent. Clarity of language, relative type size and proximity to the claim being qualified" are what make a disclosure effective. Note 3: a photo of a stone larger than life needs a true-size disclosure (relevant to hero imagery and the builder's stone renders). Same source.
- The FTC's own standard for a usable disclosure (SBP): "marketers should use plain language and sufficiently large type, place disclosures in close proximity to the qualified claim, and avoid making inconsistent statements or using distracting elements that could undercut or contradict the disclosure." SBP PDF: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/public_statements/1393857/g711001_jewelry_guides_statement_of_basis_and_purpose_for_final_jewelry__.pdf
- Tennessee mirrors Section 5 in the **Tennessee Consumer Protection Act** (Tenn. Code § 47-18-104), which lists 30+ enumerated deceptive acts and gives consumers a **private right of action** with treble damages for willful violations (§ 47-18-109). So a disclosure failure that would draw an FTC letter can also be a plaintiff's claim in Tennessee. Sources: https://www.findlaw.com/state/tennessee-law/tennessee-deceptive-trade-practices-laws.html ; https://www.tba.org/journal/the-tennessee-consumer-protection-act
- Penalty scale to keep in mind: the FTC's maximum civil penalty is **$53,088 per violation** (2025 inflation adjustment) where a rule or order applies. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-publishes-inflation-adjusted-civil-penalty-amounts-2025
- Next FTC review of the Jewelry Guides is expected **2027–2028**; the Diamond Manufacturers & Importers Association has said it will push to mandate "synthetic" for non-natural stones. Nothing is changing before the site launches, but copy should be structured so the qualifier can be swapped in one place. Source: https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/diamond-groups-upset-ftc-guides/

### 1.2 What a "diamond" is now (§ 23.12)
- 2018 definition: "A diamond is a mineral consisting essentially of pure carbon crystallized in the isometric system. It is found in many colors. Its hardness is 10; its specific gravity is approximately 3.52; and it has a refractive index of 2.42." The word **"natural" was deleted** "because lab-created products that have essentially the same optical, physical, and chemical properties as mined diamonds are also diamonds." Sources: 83 FR 40665 (above); FTC business blog https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2018/07/diamonds-and-pearls-and-revisions-ftcs-jewelry-guides
- Critical corollary, from the FTC's own 2019 guidance: **"When 'diamond' appears by itself, it still means a natural diamond."** Lab-grown product may be called a diamond, but never an *unqualified* diamond. Source (JVC column summarizing FTC staff position): https://www.nationaljeweler.com/articles/956-a-quick-review-of-the-ftc-s-rules-on-lab-grown-diamonds
- § 23.12 also says "diamond" unqualified is deceptive for a stone not "symmetrically fashioned with at least seventeen (17) polished facets" (say "rose-cut diamond"/"rough diamond" where applicable).

### 1.3 The lab-grown qualifier rule (§ 23.12 "cultured" paragraph and § 23.25(b))
- § 23.25(b): it is unfair or deceptive to use the name of any precious or semi-precious stone (or "stone," "birthstone," "gemstone") to describe a laboratory-grown, laboratory-created, [manufacturer name]-created or imitation stone **"unless such word or name is immediately preceded with equal conspicuousness by the word 'laboratory-grown,' 'laboratory-created,' '[manufacturer name]-created,' or some other word or phrase of like meaning, or by the word 'imitation' or 'simulated,' so as to disclose clearly the nature of the product and the fact it is not a mined gemstone."** Note 1 to § 23.25: "faux" is not an adequate disclosure. Sources: 83 FR 40665; https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/23.25
- § 23.25(c): using "laboratory-grown," "laboratory-created," "[manufacturer name]-created," "synthetic," or a phrase of like meaning with a stone name is deceptive unless the product "has essentially the same optical, physical, and chemical properties as the stone named." (So CZ or moissanite can never be a "laboratory-grown diamond"; they are "simulated"/"imitation.")
- The three enumerated exemplars: **laboratory-grown, laboratory-created, [manufacturer name]-created.** JVC's position: "Modifiers such as lab-grown must immediately precede the word diamond, which is a higher standard than many initially believed." Sources: https://jckonline.com/?p=102440 ; https://www.nationaljeweler.com/articles/956-a-quick-review-of-the-ftc-s-rules-on-lab-grown-diamonds
- The 2018 revision deliberately opened the list: "the final Guides advise marketers of man-made gemstones sharing the same optical, physical, and chemical properties as the named stone that they may use words or phrases immediately preceding the gemstone name other than the ones listed ... if they clearly and conspicuously disclose that the product is not a mined stone. ... Sellers remain responsible for ensuring they do not make deceptive claims that violate Section 5." (SBP, §J "Qualifying Claims About Man-Made Gemstones")

### 1.4 Is "man made diamond" acceptable? (the client's required phrase)
**Short answer: yes, with conditions.** The FTC has never listed "man-made" as an exemplar, but it has said in writing that it is usable, it uses the phrase itself as the generic term for these products, and both major labs use it. Evidence:

1. FTC SBP, verbatim: "the Guides do not limit sellers to using only the enumerated qualifiers. Rather, they advise that marketers may also use 'some other word or phrase of like meaning.' **If the commenters' suggested terms could be used non-deceptively in context (e.g., as part of an ad highlighting that the product is man-made), there is nothing to prevent marketers from doing so.**" (SBP PDF, §H.4, Cultured Diamonds — Analysis and Final Guidance.)
2. The SBP's own section headings are "Use of 'Real,' 'Genuine,' and 'Natural' to Describe **Man-Made Diamonds**" and "Qualifying Claims About **Man-Made Gemstones**" — the Commission's generic term for the category is "man-made."
3. The FTC's 2016 consumer survey evidence (cited in the SBP at the "synthetic" discussion) found that when asked to describe a "synthetic diamond," consumers most often used "fake," "**man-made**," "cubic zirconia," "cheap," and "artificial" — i.e., consumers already map "man-made" onto the not-mined category. (That same list is a caution: "man-made" lives next to "fake" in consumers' heads, which is exactly why the phrase must be paired with the word "diamond" so the net impression is "a real diamond, made by people.")
4. GIA's 2025 press release: "it is no longer relevant for GIA to describe **man-made diamonds** using the nomenclature created for ... natural diamonds." https://www.gia.edu/gia-news-press/new-descriptive-terminology-for-laboratory-grown-diamonds
5. IGI's definition: "A laboratory grown diamond has essentially the chemical, physical and optical properties as a mined diamond, with the exception of being **man-made (a manufactured product)**." https://www.igi.org/lab-grown-diamond-report
6. The counter-signal to respect: JVC's Sara Yood on non-enumerated terms, after the 2019 letters: "using [another term] is a risk." https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/ftc-lab-grown-diamond-sellers/ — and the FTC's 2019 letters criticized one company's non-standard word "aboveground" as inadequate. The difference between "aboveground" and "man-made" is that "man-made" unambiguously says the stone was manufactured; "aboveground" did not.

**Ruling for the copy deck (safest formulation):**
- Write **"man-made diamond"** / **"man-made diamonds"** — hyphenated, the qualifier **immediately before** the word diamond, **same font, size, weight and color** as the word "diamond," **every** time the product is called a diamond (titles, PDP H1, spec table, cart line, order confirmation, invoices, ads, alt text, image filenames, URLs, email subject lines, hashtags, schema `name`). Never let a tier name ("New American") stand in for the qualifier; a tier name is not a disclosure.
- On every page that sells or describes the tier, put **"laboratory-grown"** once in a factual spec line, e.g. `Origin: Man-made (laboratory-grown) diamond · IGI Laboratory Grown Diamond Report No. ____ · As grown, no post-growth treatment`. The IGI report itself is headed "LABORATORY GROWN DIAMOND" (sample report below), so the spec line simply mirrors the document the customer receives. This satisfies the client's "avoid lab-grown in our own copy" preference (it is a spec/document reference, not brand voice) while giving the site one of the three enumerated exemplars on every selling page — the belt-and-suspenders JVC would want.
- Never pair "man-made diamond" with "real," "genuine," "natural" or "precious" (§ 23.27), and never use the word "diamond" unqualified for the product anywhere on the site — including hashtags and URLs (the FTC called these out specifically in 2019).
- Hyphenation is not an FTC requirement. "Man made diamond" is permissible; "man-made diamond" is the standard English compound adjective and reads as one unit, which helps the "immediately preceding / equal conspicuousness" test. Recommend the hyphen and make it a style-guide rule.
- Brand-voice line that is safe and on-message: "A diamond grown by people, not mined — identical chemistry, graded D–F color and VVS clarity, with an IGI report."

### 1.5 The other words — "synthetic," "cultured," "real," "genuine," "natural," "lab-grown," "above-ground"
- **"Synthetic"** — dropped from the recommended qualifier list for diamonds in 2018 because "many consumers mistakenly believe 'synthetic' means an artificial product such as cubic zirconia" (SBP). Not prohibited, but the Commission "cautions marketers that it would be deceptive to use the terms 'real,' 'genuine,' 'natural,' or 'synthetic' to imply that a lab-grown diamond ... is not, in fact, an actual diamond." Do not use "synthetic" for your own product; do not use it to disparage anyone else's.
- **"Cultured"** — allowed only if qualified so that the net impression is "not mined." The FTC's 2012 survey: 53% of respondents thought a "cultured diamond" was a natural product; with "laboratory-created" added, 13%. The 2019 letters faulted a seller for labeling some stones "cultured" and others "lab grown," which implied "cultured" meant mined. Recommendation: do not use "cultured" at all. Sources: SBP; https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2019/04/warning-letters-re-mined-diamond-sellers-describe-products-accurately
- **"Real," "genuine," "natural," "precious," "semi-precious"** — § 23.27: unfair or deceptive to use these for any product "manufactured or produced artificially." The FTC kept this in 2018 and invited consumer-perception evidence for a future petition; none has changed it. NAD (2021) also told the Natural Diamond Council that using "real" to distinguish mined from lab-grown misleadingly implies lab-grown stones are not diamonds — so the word is dangerous in both directions: never "real diamond" for the man-made tier, and in Americana copy prefer "natural diamond"/"mined diamond" over "real diamond." Sources: 83 FR 40665; SBP §I; https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/watchdog-natural-diamond-council/ ; https://bbbprograms.org/media-center/press-releases-details/nad-natural-diamond-council-claims
- **"Lab-grown" / "lab-created"** — the abbreviation is treated by the FTC and JVC as a phrase of like meaning (the FTC's own 2019 blog and letters use "lab-grown"); the IGDA survey cited in the SBP found 77% of consumers correctly read "lab-grown diamonds" as "created in above-ground facilities." If you ever use the abbreviation, it is safe. The site will use "man-made" + "laboratory-grown" (spec), so the abbreviation is unnecessary.
- **"Above-ground," "foundry," "grown"** alone — flagged in 2019 ("aboveground") as inadequate. Avoid as sole qualifiers.
- **"Diamond simulant" words** — "simulated"/"imitation" are for CZ/moissanite only; never near the man-made tier.

### 1.6 Proximity and conspicuousness: what the 2019 FTC letters actually said
The FTC sent staff warning letters (April 2019) to eight sellers — Diamond Foundry, Pure Grown Diamonds, Ada Diamonds (lab-grown) and Agape Diamonds, Diamond Nexus, MiaDonna, Stauer, Timepieces International (simulants). Findings that translate directly into site rules:
- Disclosures "**proximate to individual advertising or product descriptions**" are required; "consumers could easily overlook general webpages when reviewing individual product descriptions." A "diamond education" page or FAQ does not cure an unqualified PDP title. "The closer the disclosure is to the claim to which it relates, the better."
- The Guides apply to **social media**: beware "#diamonds" hashtags and domain names containing "diamond" for non-mined product.
- Do not mix "cultured" and "lab grown" inconsistently.
- Unqualified eco-benefit claims ("eco-friendly," "eco-conscious," "sustainable") are not acceptable (see Part 3).
- Recipients had 10 days to respond with revisions. Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2019/04/warning-letters-re-mined-diamond-sellers-describe-products-accurately ; https://www.jckonline.com/magazine-article/ftc-lab-grown-diamond-sellers/ ; https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/ftc-lab-grown-diamonds/

### 1.7 Treatment disclosure, including post-growth treatment of man-made diamonds
- § 23.14 (diamonds) sends you to § 23.24 (gemstones): disclose a treatment when it (a) "is not permanent," (b) "creates special care requirements," or (c) "has a significant effect on the stone's value." The Note says this applies "at all trade levels" and, for remote sales (catalog, online, TV), the disclosure must be made **before purchase**. Source: 83 FR 40665.
- Many CVD-grown diamonds are HPHT-processed after growth to improve color. IGI prints it in the comments line — a 2025 IGI report reads: "As Grown - No indication of post-growth treatment. This Laboratory Grown Diamond was created by High Pressure High Temperature (HPHT) growth process." Other reports read "Indications of post-growth treatment." Source: IGI report LG689582130 https://pdf.igi.org/ID689582130.pdf ; GIA's colored LGD reports include "an assessment of growth type and post-growth treatment" https://www.gia.edu/laboratory-grown-diamond
- Site rule: the PDP spec for every man-made stone carries the report's growth method and treatment line verbatim ("As grown" or "Post-growth treated"). For the New American tier, the cleanest promise is to buy "as grown" stones only and say so; if you ever sell post-growth-treated stones, disclose it on the PDP.
- Natural-diamond treatments (laser drilling, fracture filling, HPHT color processing, irradiation) must likewise be disclosed on the PDP because they significantly affect value; "clarity-enhanced" is a disclosure, not a feature.

### 1.8 Enforcement history 2019 → 2026 (what has actually happened)
- **2019** — the eight FTC staff warning letters above (origin disclosure + Green Guides). No civil penalties; letters only.
- **2021 (NAD, not FTC)** — BBB National Programs' NAD told **Diamond Foundry** to disclose origin more prominently and fix social posts (April 2021), then told the **Natural Diamond Council** (April 22, 2021) to drop claims that lab-grown emits three times the carbon of mined, that lab-grown has "little to no resale value," scarcity/urgency claims, and the use of "real" to contrast mined with man-made. Sources: https://bbbprograms.org/media-center/press-releases-details/nad-natural-diamond-council-claims ; https://rapaport.com/news/diamond-foundry-gets-warning-over-ads/ ; https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/watchdog-natural-diamond-council/
- **2024–2026** — this research found **no new FTC warning letters or cases specifically about lab-grown diamond marketing** after 2019. The FTC's active enforcement priorities touching jewelry in this window are (i) the **Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule** (effective Oct 21, 2024; civil penalties), and (ii) **Made in USA** — a coordinated sweep on April 14, 2026 (three actions, including a $2M judgment and a $625,000 redress order) and seven warning letters on July 6, 2026 that treated "handmade in Austin, Texas," "#madeincali," "BUILT IN THE USA" and "created by American workers" as unqualified origin claims. Both are directly relevant to this brand (Part 2 and Part 4). Sources: https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/made-in-the-usa-prove-it-ftc-marks-americas-250th-with-crack-down-on-domestic-origin-claims ; https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/consumer-products-watch/made-texas-hold-your-horses-ftc-fires-more-made-the-usa ; https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ftc-sends-more-warning-letters-over-made-in-usa-claims.md
- **Lab-side change with marketing consequences:** GIA stopped issuing natural-style color/clarity grades for lab-grown diamonds on **October 1, 2025** and now issues "Premium" / "Standard" (see §4.1). A "D–F, VVS" promise is therefore an **IGI** (or other lab) promise, not a GIA one.

### 1.9 Gold, karat, "solid," plating, rhodium (§§ 23.3, 23.4, 23.7)
- Unqualified "gold" means 24K. A karat alloy must carry the karat **immediately before** "gold" with equal conspicuousness ("14 Karat Gold," "14K Gold"). Since 2018, alloys **below 10K may be called gold** if the karat is disclosed the same way ("9 Kt. Gold"). Sources: 83 FR 40665 § 23.3; https://nationaljeweler.com/articles/4826-8-key-changes-the-ftc-made-to-the-jewelry-guides-a-refresher
- **"Solid"**: the Guides prohibit "solid" for hollow-center pieces ("14 Karat Gold-Hollow Center," never "14 Kt. Solid Gold"). "Solid gold" otherwise has no special definition; because "gold" unqualified implies 24K, write "solid 14K gold," never "solid gold" alone. Avoid "solid" on plated or filled pieces.
- Plating terms and minimums (§ 23.3): mechanical "gold plate"/"gold overlay" ≥ 1/40 of total metal weight of ≥10K alloy; "gold filled" ≥ 1/20 by weight; "gold electroplate" ≥ 0.175 µm fine gold; "heavy gold electroplate" ≥ 2.5 µm. Karat of the plating layer must precede the term ("14K Gold Electroplate"). "Vermeil" (§ 23.4) = sterling base + ≥10K gold ≥ 2.5 µm. "Reasonable durability" on all significant surfaces is required for any plating claim.
- **Rhodium** (§ 23.7, new in 2018): a rhodium surface layer on a precious-metal product (typical for white gold) should be disclosed — consumers don't know it wears and must be reapplied. Put "rhodium-plated white gold; re-plating is normal maintenance" in the metal spec and the care page.
- Weight (§ 23.18): if a carat figure is the total of several stones, say "total weight"/"ct. t.w." with equal conspicuousness, and give the center-stone weight separately.
- "Flawless"/"perfect" (§ 23.13) only for stones with no internal or external imperfections at 10×; "blue white" (§ 23.15) is effectively banned.

### 1.10 "Handmade," "hand-crafted," "hand-finished" (§ 23.2) — and what CAD/CAM + casting can say
- § 23.2(a): deceptive to call a product "handmade or hand-wrought" unless "the entire shaping and forming of such product from raw materials and its finishing and decoration were accomplished by hand labor and manually-controlled methods which permit the maker to control and vary the construction, shape, design, and finish of each part of each individual product." Raw materials = "bulk sheet, strip, wire, precious metal clays, ingots, casting grain, and similar items." § 23.2(b): "hand-forged, hand-engraved, hand-finished, or hand-polished" are fine **if that specific operation** was done by hand with manual control. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/23.2
- In 2018 JVC asked the FTC to add "An industry product that has been hand-cast from hand-carved or hand-modeled wax or hand-cast from handmade molds may be described as 'handmade.'" The Commission **declined**: "The Commission declines to add language addressing hand-cast jewelry to Section 23.2(a) because it lacks evidence that such guidance would prevent deception" (JVC's own survey showed ~34% of consumers did not consider a cast-from-hand-carved-wax, hand-finished ring "handmade"). (SBP §P.) CAD-modeled, cast pieces are therefore **not "handmade"** under the Guides, and the July 2026 FTC letters show "handmade in [place]" is read literally.
- What is safe and true for American Jewelry's process: "designed in Tennessee," "designed in-house," "cast and finished in Brentwood, Tennessee" (when true), "hand-set," "hand-finished," "hand-polished," "hand-engraved" (when those operations are manual), "bench-made in our Brentwood workshop," "made to order." Reserve "handmade"/"hand-fabricated" for pieces actually formed from sheet/wire/ingot by hand (some Royal American one-of-ones may qualify — document the process per piece).

## Part 2 — "Made in USA" and what a Tennessee jeweler using imported stones and gold can say

### 2.1 The standard and the 2021 Rule
- Unqualified U.S.-origin claims require "all or virtually all" U.S. content: the FTC's Enforcement Policy Statement and the **Made in USA Labeling Rule, 16 CFR Part 323** (final July 1, 2021). § 323.2: it is deceptive "to label any product as Made in the United States unless the final assembly or processing of the product occurs in the United States, all significant processing that goes into the product occurs in the United States, and all or virtually all ingredients or components of the product are made and sourced in the United States." § 323.1 defines "Made in the United States" as "**any unqualified representation, express or implied**, that a product or service, or a specified component thereof, is of U.S. origin," and reaches "mail order promotional material" — "materials ... disseminated in print or by electronic means" that solicit a purchase without examining the product (i.e., your website and emails). Sources: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/323.2 ; https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/323.1 ; https://www.ftc.gov/made-in-usa-rule
- Rule violations carry civil penalties (up to $53,088 per violation); recent outcomes: Williams-Sonoma $3.17M (2024), Kubota $2M (2024), and the April 2026 sweep. Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/made-in-usa-rule ; Crowell alert above.
- **State and city claims count.** The July 2026 letters treated "Made in Texas" and "handmade in Austin, Texas" the same as "Made in USA"; hashtags count too (#madeinusa, #madeincali, #americanmadeus). "Manufactured," "built," and "created" in the USA were all read as unqualified origin claims. So "Made in Tennessee," "Tennessee-made," "#madeintennessee" are governed by the same all-or-virtually-all test. Sources: AFS Law and Kelley Drye posts above.
- The FTC's compliance guide uses **gold in a ring as its example** of a raw material that defeats an unqualified claim: imported gold is a significant component by value and function, so a U.S.-cast ring of imported gold cannot be "Made in USA" without qualification. Imported diamonds are, a fortiori, significant components. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-made-usa-standard

### 2.2 Qualified claims a jeweler with imported diamonds and gold can truthfully make
The FTC accepts qualified claims that are accurate and not misleading: "Made in USA of U.S. and imported parts," "60% U.S. content," "Assembled in USA" (only where the assembly is substantial, not "screwdriver" assembly), and specific-process claims like "Designed in USA," which "must clearly reference only that specific process, not overall manufacture." Source: complying-made-usa-standard (above).

For American Jewelry, truthful formulations by stage of the in-house build-out:

| Stage of operations | Safe claim | Do not say |
|---|---|---|
| Designs in-house; mountings cast by a U.S. or foreign caster; stones imported; set/finished in Brentwood | "Designed in Tennessee. Set and finished in our Brentwood workshop. Diamonds and gold are imported." | "Made in USA," "Made in Tennessee," "American-made," "#madeinusa," "Assembled in USA" (unless casting is also domestic and substantial) |
| Designs + casts + sets + finishes in-house in TN; gold and stones imported | "Designed, cast and finished in Tennessee, USA, with imported diamonds and gold" / "Made in USA of imported materials" | Unqualified "Made in USA"/"Made in Tennessee"; "100% American" |
| Grows its own diamonds in TN; still imports gold | "Diamonds grown in Tennessee, USA" (a specified-component claim, fine if the growth happens there); jewelry: "Made in USA with imported gold" or "Designed, grown, cast and finished in Tennessee with imported gold" | "Made in USA" unqualified for the finished jewelry |
| U.S.-refined gold with documented U.S. origin + TN-grown stones + all work in TN | Unqualified "Made in USA" becomes defensible — only with documentation per component (refiner origin certificates) | — |

Notes: (1) "American Jewelry" as the trade name, tier names "Royal American / Americana / New American," and a stated "made in America" ambition together create an **implied-origin context**; put the qualified statement on the About/Craft page and keep an "Origin" line on every PDP so no page implies more than is true. (2) The qualifier must be **clear and conspicuous** and near the claim, not in a footer. (3) Recycled-gold or "American-refined gold" claims need refiner documentation (see Part 3).

## Part 3 — Environmental and ethical claims about man-made diamonds

### 3.1 Green Guides (16 CFR Part 260) constraints
- § 260.4 **General environmental benefit claims**: "It is deceptive to misrepresent, directly or by implication, that a product ... offers a general environmental benefit." Unqualified claims like "eco-friendly," "green," and (per the FTC's 2019 letters) "sustainable" are "difficult to interpret and likely convey that the product ... has specific and far-reaching environmental benefits" that cannot be substantiated. Marketers may qualify with "clear and prominent qualifying language that limits the claim to a specific benefit." Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/260.4
- § 260.3 general principles: qualifications must be clear, prominent, close to the claim; don't overstate; comparative claims need a clear basis. (Cited in SBP note 354.)
- § 260.13 **Recycled content**: unqualified "made from recycled material" only if "the entire product or package, excluding minor, incidental components, is made from recycled material"; content must come "from the waste stream, either during the manufacturing process (pre-consumer), or after consumer use (post-consumer)"; manufacturing scrap normally reused in-house is not "recycled." "Recycled gold" claims need refiner certification (e.g., SCS Recycled Content) and should state the percentage and pre-/post-consumer basis. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/260.13
- § 260.15 renewable-energy claims and § 260.5 carbon-offset claims apply if the grower claims renewable power or carbon neutrality — those need the grower's evidence, not a brochure line.
- The 2019 FTC letters are the only FTC action on diamond eco-claims: lab-grown sellers "should not use unqualified eco-benefit claims such as eco-friendly, eco-conscious, or sustainable"; "if a company is claiming its product is 'eco-friendly' because it's recyclable, or because it takes measures to use less energy or less water in the manufacturing process, then the ad has to state this." Sources: FTC 2019 blog; National Jeweler JVC column (above).
- NAD's 2021 decision rejected the mined-side comparison ("3× carbon") for unreliable evidence and found the implied message "mined diamonds are better for the environment than man-made" unsupported — meaning **neither side's generic comparison is substantiated**. Don't make one. Source: https://bbbprograms.org/media-center/press-releases-details/nad-natural-diamond-council-claims
- The Green Guides have been under review since December 2022 (comments closed April 2023); no final revision as of this memo. Build copy to the current Guides. Source: https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/ftc-seeks-public-comment-on-possible-2835749

### 3.2 "Conflict-free," "ethically sourced," Russian-origin
- "Conflict-free" in the trade means sourced through the **Kimberley Process Certification Scheme** with a **System of Warranties** statement on each B2B invoice. KP covers rough diamonds and "conflict" is defined narrowly (rebel-funded conflict); the System of Warranties is self-declared. Retailers are expected to insist suppliers provide the warranty statement on invoices and to retain them for five years. Source: https://dmcc.ae/ecosystems/diamonds/systems-of-warranties ; https://jckonline.com/?p=40413
- For man-made diamonds, "conflict-free" is literally true but functions as an implied comparative/ethical superiority claim; the factual, unarguable statement is "not mined." If you say "conflict-free" for natural stones, qualify it: "sourced through suppliers who provide Kimberley Process System of Warranties statements on every invoice."
- **Russian diamonds**: U.S. imports of non-industrial diamonds of Russian origin are banned (EO 14068; OFAC determinations effective March 1, 2024 for ≥1.0 ct and September 1, 2024 for ≥0.5 ct, including stones cut elsewhere). Importers of record must upload a **self-certification** that stones ≥0.5 ct were "not mined, extracted, produced, or manufactured wholly or in part in the Russian Federation." A retailer buying from U.S. dealers is not the importer, but any "ethically sourced" or "non-Russian" claim needs supplier representations in writing. Sources: https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/1164 ; https://ofac.treasury.gov/system/files/2024-02/determination_02082024_eo14068.pdf ; https://rapaport.com/news/us-diamond-importers-can-self-certify-mining-source-at-point-of-entry-says-jvc/
- "Ethically sourced," "responsibly sourced" are general benefit claims; substantiate with specific, verifiable facts (RJC-certified supplier, named mine/producer, SoW statements) and say the specific thing instead of the slogan.

### 3.3 What can be claimed with substantiation (copy that survives)
- "Grown in a laboratory [in country/state] by [grower], using [X]% renewable electricity" — only with the grower's documentation (and watch § 260.15 if RECs are involved).
- "Not mined." (Factual; the best single ethical line for the man-made tier.)
- "Recycled 14K gold, [X]% post-consumer, certified by [refiner/standard]."
- "Our natural diamonds come with Kimberley Process System of Warranties statements from our suppliers." (If true and the invoices are on file.)
- Nothing generic: no "sustainable," "eco-friendly," "green," "ethical," "carbon-neutral," "guilt-free," "earth-friendly," and no leaf/earth iconography next to the man-made tier (imagery can make an implied claim — § 260.4 Example 3, the printer-in-a-bird's-nest).

## Part 4 — Trust content a luxury jewelry site must have

### 4.1 Grading reports: GIA vs IGI, inscription, and the October 2025 change
- **Natural diamonds** — GIA is the reference lab; GIA reports state the stone is natural and the report number is laser-inscribed on the girdle. Offer "GIA-graded" only when the specific stone has a GIA report; "GIA-certified" is loose (GIA does not "certify"), say "GIA report."
- **Man-made diamonds** — IGI has graded them since 2005 and issues full 4Cs letter grades (color, clarity, cut, polish, symmetry, fluorescence), states the growth method (CVD/HPHT), states "As Grown" or post-growth treatment, and laser-inscribes the report number (prefix LG) on the girdle; the report is headed "LABORATORY GROWN DIAMOND REPORT" and is verifiable at igi.org. Sources: https://www.igi.org/lab-grown-diamond-report ; https://pdf.igi.org/ID689582130.pdf
- **GIA changed the rules on October 1, 2025**: GIA "will no longer use the color and clarity nomenclature that GIA developed for natural diamonds" for lab-grown; its Laboratory-Grown Diamond Quality Assessment now returns **"Premium"** (per GIA: VVS-or-better clarity, D color, Excellent polish/symmetry, Excellent cut for rounds) or **"Standard"** (VS clarity, E–J color, Very Good polish/symmetry, Very Good cut), with below-Standard stones receiving no designation. The girdle is inscribed "Laboratory-Grown" plus the assessment number. Sources: https://www.gia.edu/gia-news-press/new-descriptive-terminology-for-laboratory-grown-diamonds ; https://www.gia.edu/gia-news-press/updated-laboratory-grown-diamond-services-to-launch-october-1 ; https://www.gia.edu/laboratory-grown-diamond
  - Consequence for the **New American tier spec ("D–F color, VVS clarity, IGI reports")**: the spec is coherent only with IGI (or another letter-grading lab). Under GIA's system an E or F VVS stone would read "Standard," which would undercut the tier's promise. Keep IGI as the tier's lab and say "graded by IGI" — don't say or imply GIA. Verify GIA's exact Premium/Standard criteria on gia.edu before writing any comparison.
  - Avoid claiming IGI and GIA grading are "equivalent"; the trade press reports GIA grades more conservatively on some stones. Source: https://pricescope.com/education/igi-vs-gia-lab-grown-diamond-certification
- PDP trust block: lab name, report type, report number, **"Verify this report" link** (GIA Report Check / IGI verify), inscription statement ("report number laser-inscribed on the girdle"), growth method + treatment line (man-made), and a scan/PDF of the report at delivery.

### 4.2 Appraisal documents
- Insurance appraisals are written at **retail replacement value** by a credentialed appraiser (Jewelers Mutual points to AGS, NAJA and GIA credentials); they should include a full description, photos, metal and stone details, report numbers and the value; update every ~2 years. A sales receipt or grading report is supporting documentation, not a substitute. Source: https://ode1.jewelersmutual.com/jewelry-insurance-explained/appraisals
- Do **not** use appraisals as price anchors ("appraised at $8,000, yours for $4,500"). The FTC's Guides Against Deceptive Pricing (16 CFR Part 233) require any comparison price to be a bona fide price at which the item was actually offered/sold; an inflated "appraised value" is a fictitious comparison. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/part-233
- Recommend: an appraisal is included with every piece over a stated threshold, prepared by a named GIA GG on staff (or an independent appraiser), and the site explains the difference between purchase price and insurance replacement value.

### 4.3 Warranty and lifetime care (Magnuson-Moss)
- Any **written warranty** on a consumer product over $10 must be designated **"Full"** or **"Limited"**; over $15 it must disclose, in a single clear document: what's covered/excluded, duration, what you will do (repair/replace/refund), how to get service, limitations, and "This warranty gives you specific legal rights, and you may also have other rights which vary from state to state." "Lifetime" must say whose life (the product's, the original owner's ownership). You cannot condition the warranty on using your own service unless it is free (tie-in rule), though you may exclude damage caused by third-party work. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/businesspersons-guide-federal-warranty-law
- **Pre-sale availability (16 CFR 702.3)**: online sellers must make the full warranty text available before purchase — "in close proximity to the warranted product (including through electronic or other means)" — i.e., a link on the PDP to the full warranty text, plus a non-internet way to request a copy. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/702.3
- Industry norms (Blue Nile, 25karats and similar): manufacturing defects covered for life of original owner; **exclusions** for loss, theft, wear, damage, stone loss; many condition stone-loss coverage on **inspection every six months** with a signed record; free cleaning/inspection, prong re-tipping and rhodium re-plating are typically "care" services, some paid after year one. Source: https://www.25karats.com/lifetime-warranty ; https://www.bluenile.com/services/warranty
- Recommend splitting into two documents: **"Limited Lifetime Warranty"** (manufacturing defects, original owner, Mag-Moss compliant) and a **"Lifetime Care Program"** (what you do for free forever: cleaning, inspection, re-tipping on inspection schedule, one re-plate per year, etc.) so marketing "lifetime care" is not itself a warranty promise you can't define.

### 4.4 Returns, exchanges, custom/made-to-order, resizing
- No federal law mandates returns; the **Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule (16 CFR Part 435)** requires a reasonable basis for any stated ship time, a 30-day default when none is stated, delay notices with the right to cancel for a prompt refund. For made-to-order, state the production time on the PDP and at checkout, and build the delay-notice flow. Source: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule
- Norms from the online leaders: **30 days** from shipment/availability (Brilliant Earth, Blue Nile); items unworn, original condition, with grading reports and packaging; **custom, engraved, personalized, resized-after-purchase, special-order, upgrade-program and "final sale" items are non-returnable** (Brilliant Earth allows engraved returns for a $40 removal fee); free insured return label (Brilliant Earth both ways; Blue Nile two free labels/year, then $30). Resizing: one complimentary resize within 60 days (any size) or up to a year within a stated range; eternity bands, milgrain/ornate and alternative-metal rings excluded. Sources: https://www.brilliantearth.com/30-Day-Returns ; https://www.bluenile.com/policies/returns
- Local fine jewelers more commonly run 14–30 days for stock, exchange-only on sale items, and deposit-based custom work with the deposit non-refundable after CAD approval — document the approval gate (the customer signs off a render) so "final sale" is anchored to a decision the customer made.
- Tennessee: this research did not find a Tennessee statute requiring posted refund policies (some states have one). Post the policy conspicuously anyway and link it at checkout; confirm with counsel.

### 4.5 Shipping and insurance
- **FedEx** caps declared value for jewelry at **$1,000 per package** under standard terms; the **Declared Value Advantage** program (application required) allows up to **$100,000** domestic and provides security packaging; FedEx says plainly that declared value "is not insurance." Pack double-boxed, never write "jewelry" on the label, no padded envelopes over $500, and request **Adult Signature**. Source: https://www.fedex.com/en-us/shipping/how-to-ship-jewelry.html
- **Jewelers Mutual JM Shipping Solution** provides discounted FedEx/UPS/USPS labels with full or excess coverage for the trade (Shopify app launched Sept 2025). Source: https://www.nationaljeweler.com/articles/14276-jewelers-mutual-launches-shipping-solution-app-for-shopify
- USPS Registered Mail (insurable to $50,000) and armored/specialty carriers (Brink's, Malca-Amit) are the trade's options for very high values; pick one and write it into the Shipping page (verify current limits with the carrier).
- Policy page content: insured both directions, signature required on all orders (adult signature over $X), no P.O. boxes for high-value, ship-to-store option for pickup with ID, title/risk pass on delivery signature, what to do if a package arrives damaged (photograph before opening, 48-hour window).

### 4.6 Pricing transparency
- Show prices on PDPs for Americana and New American; "price upon request" only for Royal American one-of-ones (expected in high jewelry). Price-anchoring must satisfy 16 CFR Part 233 (bona fide former prices; no fictitious MSRP; no appraisal-as-comparison). Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/part-233
- Show tax/shipping before the final step; the FTC's 2025 "junk fees" rule covers only live-event tickets and short-term lodging, but Section 5 still reaches hidden mandatory fees.
- Man-made vs natural: do **not** publish comparative "value retention," "resale," or "investment" statements for either tier — NAD found the Natural Diamond Council's "no resale value" claims unsupported, and the inverse ("holds value") is unsupported too.

### 4.7 Financing disclosure (Affirm)
Affirm's compliance guidance (TILA/Reg Z "trigger terms"):
- If you advertise an "as low as $X/mo" payment, you must also give the **APR and term length** no more than **one click away**.
- If any interest-bearing terms exist, write **"As low as 0% APR"**, "Starting at 0% APR," or "From 0% APR" — not "0% APR" alone; Affirm's own sample avoids "interest-free."
- Required qualifiers: **"subject to an eligibility check"**, "Affirm availability and eligibility may vary," "Restrictions apply," and the lender/CA disclosures. Sample disclosure: "Your rate will be 0–36% APR based on credit, and is subject to an eligibility check." Payment example format: "At a purchase price of $400 you could pay a down payment of $80, followed by 12 monthly payments of $28.88 at 15% APR."
- Never "no credit check," "guaranteed approval," or "everyone qualifies."
Sources: https://affirm.com/business/blog/marketing-compliant ; https://businesshub.affirm.com/hc/en-us/articles/6402545332628 (Affirm Business Hub, Marketing compliance). Get the current U.S. Marketing Compliance Guide from Affirm's merchant portal before launch (the attachment links are gated).

### 4.8 Accessibility (ADA Title III) — current status
- There is still **no DOJ Title III web regulation**; the DOJ's web rulemaking for public accommodations has sat on the "inactive" list since 2017 and remains inactive in the 2025 Unified Agenda. The April 2024 DOJ rule (WCAG 2.1 AA) applies only to **state and local governments** (Title II). Courts, however, apply Title III to retailer websites, and WCAG 2.1 AA is the de facto standard. Sources: https://www.levelaccess.com/doj-puts-draft-digital-accessibility-rules-inactive-list/ ; https://www.consumerfinancemonitor.com/2017/08/02/businesses-continue-to-face-uncertainty-litigation-risk-in-the-wake-of-dojs-inactive-web-accessibility-rulemaking/
- Litigation is up: **3,117 federal website-accessibility suits in 2025 (+27% vs 2024)**, 36% of all ADA Title III federal filings; e-commerce/retail is ~70–77% of cases; New York courts busiest; Shopify stores are over-represented. Sources: https://www.adatitleiii.com/2026/03/federal-court-website-accessibility-lawsuit-filings-bounce-back-in-2025/ ; https://testparty.ai/blog/ada-lawsuit-trends-ecommerce-2025-2026-data
- Build to **WCAG 2.2 AA** (keyboard-operable builder and checkout, alt text on every product image, contrast, focus states, captions on video), publish an **Accessibility Statement** with a contact path, and do not rely on an overlay widget (overlays are themselves a lawsuit magnet). Physical stores mean the "nexus" theory applies regardless of circuit.

### 4.9 Privacy and cookies (Tennessee + U.S.)
- **Tennessee Information Protection Act (TIPA)**, effective **July 1, 2025**, applies only to businesses with **>$25M revenue AND** (175,000+ consumers' data, or 25,000+ with >50% revenue from selling data). A two-store jeweler is almost certainly below the threshold — confirm revenue. If it ever applies: privacy-notice contents, rights to access/correct/delete/port, opt-out of targeted advertising/sale/profiling, data-protection assessments, processor contracts; AG-only enforcement, 60-day cure, up to $7,500/violation (treble if willful), NIST Privacy Framework affirmative defense; no universal opt-out signal requirement. Sources: https://www.bakerdonelson.com/consumer-data-privacy-law-guide-tennessee ; https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/tennessee-passes-comprehensive-data-privacy-law
- Even below TIPA: (i) **CalOPPA** requires any commercial site collecting personal information from California residents to post a privacy policy (practically every U.S. e-commerce site); (ii) **CCPA** applies only above $26.625M revenue / 100,000 CA consumers / 50% data-sale revenue — likely not applicable, but Meta Pixel/Google Ads remarketing count as "sharing" under CCPA-type laws if a threshold is ever met; (iii) the FTC Act makes any privacy-policy promise enforceable; (iv) **CAN-SPAM** for email and **TCPA** prior-express-written-consent for marketing SMS; (v) Tennessee's breach-notification statute (Tenn. Code § 47-18-2107) applies to customer data. Source for CCPA thresholds: https://www.clym.io/blog/ccpa-applicability-guide
- Cookie banners are not legally required in Tennessee. Recommended minimum: a privacy policy that names the pixels/analytics in use, an email/SMS consent checkbox with the TCPA language, a "Do Not Sell or Share" link only if a state law ever applies, and honoring GPC if you ever sell to covered states.

### 4.10 Reviews — the FTC rule and Google's rules
- **FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465), effective Oct 21, 2024**: bans fake/AI-generated reviews, buying positive or negative reviews, **incentives conditioned on sentiment** (you may incentivize a review, not a 5-star review), undisclosed insider/employee/relative reviews, company-controlled "independent" review sites, **review suppression** (legal threats, intimidation, and representing that displayed reviews are all/most reviews when negative ones are hidden), and fake social-media indicators. Civil penalties apply. Sources: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials ; https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-08-22/html/2024-18519.htm
- **Google review-snippet structured data**: "If the entity that's being reviewed controls the reviews about itself, their pages that use LocalBusiness or any other type of Organization structured data are ineligible for star review feature" — so **do not put `aggregateRating` from Google reviews on the JewelryStore/Organization schema**; "Don't aggregate reviews or ratings from other websites"; ratings must come from users. Product-level `aggregateRating` is allowed only for reviews collected on your own site for that product. Source: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet
- Displaying Google reviews on the site: use the Places API (or a widget that does) and follow its display policy — Google Maps logo/"Google Maps" attribution, always credit the reviewer (avatar, name, profile link), display attributions, and don't cache content beyond allowed exceptions (place IDs may be stored). Disclose any filtering/sorting. Source: https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/policies
- Review solicitation: ask every customer the same way (no gating by sentiment), never pay for reviews, and have staff disclose their relationship if they ever post.

### (b) Required disclosures checklist by page type

**Every page (global)**
- Qualifier discipline: no unqualified "diamond" for the man-made tier anywhere, including nav labels, footer, meta titles, OG tags, URLs, image alt/filenames.
- Footer links: Privacy, Terms, Shipping & Insurance, Returns & Exchanges, Warranty & Care, Accessibility, Financing disclosure.
- Any "$X/mo" badge → APR range + term + "subject to an eligibility check" within one click.

**Tier pages (Royal American / Americana / New American)**
- First sentence of New American states origin plainly ("man-made diamonds, grown in a laboratory, not mined") and the lab ("graded by IGI").
- Spec line with "laboratory-grown" once; growth method/treatment statement; D–F / VVS promise tied to IGI.
- No environmental/ethical slogans; a "How it's made" paragraph with documented facts only.
- Royal American: define "one-of-one" (never reproduced; design retired), describe process honestly (CAD → cast → hand-set/finished, or hand-fabricated when true).
- Origin paragraph: the qualified Tennessee statement from Part 2.

**PDP**
- Title and H1 with the qualifier; metal with karat (and "rhodium-plated" for white gold); stone origin; lab + report type + number + verify link; inscription note; growth method + treatment; carat (center vs t.w.); color/clarity/cut; treatment disclosure for natural stones; ring sizes available and resizability (e.g., "eternity style — not resizable"); production/ship time (Mail Order Rule); return eligibility badge ("30-day returns" vs "made to order — final sale after CAD approval"); link to full warranty text (702.3); financing line with eligibility qualifier; photo-scale note where renders are enlarged (§ 23.1 Note 3).

**Builder (custom / made-to-order)**
- Stone selector labels "Natural diamond" vs "Man-made diamond" on every stone card and in the summary, never a bare "Diamond."
- Running spec panel repeats origin, lab, report number per selected stone.
- Approval gate: render sign-off checkbox with "custom pieces are final sale once approved; one complimentary resize within 60 days" and the production-time statement.
- Deposit terms and what is refundable before approval.

**Cart / checkout / order confirmation**
- Line items carry the qualifier and metal karat (receipts are marketing materials under § 23.0).
- Ship-time statement per item; signature-required notice; insurance statement.
- Affirm block with the full required disclosure; no "0% APR" alone.
- Privacy/consent: marketing email checkbox (unchecked), SMS consent with TCPA language (separate, unchecked).
- Links to Returns, Warranty, Terms before the pay button; Mail Order Rule delay flow wired into order status emails.

**Policy pages** — see (c). **Store pages** — see (d).

### (c) Policy pages the site needs — recommended terms

1. **Returns & Exchanges** — 30 days from delivery/pickup for stock pieces, unworn, original condition, with grading report, appraisal and packaging; free insured return label via the store (FedEx staffed location, double-boxed); refund to original tender within ~5 business days of inspection. **Final sale**: custom/made-to-order after render approval, engraved/personalized, resized-after-purchase, special orders, pre-owned watches (or a shorter 7-day window with authentication re-check), trade-in/upgrade transactions, "final sale" items. Exchange/store credit option for sale items. State clearly that returns of financed orders refund to Affirm.
2. **Ring Sizing & Resizing** — one complimentary resize within 60 days of delivery (up to ±1 size), reasonable-fee resizing thereafter for life; exclusions (eternity bands, certain patterned/alternative-metal rings); resized rings become non-returnable; free sizing consultation in store.
3. **Limited Lifetime Warranty** — Mag-Moss compliant: "Limited," original owner, manufacturing defects in materials and workmanship for the life of the original owner's ownership; excludes loss, theft, wear, damage, chipped/lost stones not caused by a defect, work by others that causes damage; service by returning to either store; "This warranty gives you specific legal rights…"; full text linked from every PDP; printed copy on request.
4. **Lifetime Care Program** (separate from the warranty) — free cleaning and inspection anytime, documented inspections every 6 months recommended, prong re-tipping and rhodium re-plating terms, what is free vs paid, and that stone-loss coverage (if offered at all) is conditioned on the inspection record.
5. **Shipping & Insurance** — carriers, insured both ways, adult-signature required, no P.O. boxes above $X, ship-to-store with ID, production and transit times, title/risk on delivery, damaged-package steps, international (if any) duties/taxes.
6. **Custom & Made-to-Order Terms** — consultation → quote → deposit (amount, refundable until render approval) → render approval (final sale from here) → production time → balance due → delivery; change-order pricing; what the customer owns (design IP stays with the house unless agreed); one-of-one exclusivity promise for Royal American.
7. **Pre-Owned Rolex Terms** — authentication process, condition grading, what "unworn/complete set" means, service history, warranty (your own, not Rolex's), non-affiliation statement, trade-in/consignment terms, ID and anti-money-laundering requirements for high-value cash transactions.
8. **Appraisal Policy** — what's included with purchase, retail replacement value explained, appraiser credentials, update cadence, fee for third-party pieces.
9. **Financing Disclosure** — Affirm's required disclosure text, lender list link, CA notice, eligibility language.
10. **Privacy Policy** (CalOPPA-grade: data collected, pixels/analytics named, purposes, sharing, retention, consumer contact, children, security, breach notice) + **Terms of Service** (governing law Tennessee, order acceptance, pricing errors, IP, review/UGC terms, dispute resolution — ask counsel re: arbitration).
11. **Accessibility Statement** — WCAG 2.2 AA target, known gaps, contact channel, in-store assistance.
12. **Product Information & Diamond Disclosures** — a plain-English page explaining natural vs man-made, grading labs, treatments, metals/plating, weights, and the words used on the site (this is the "education" page the FTC says is *not a substitute* for PDP disclosures but is still valuable).

### (e) Open legal questions for a lawyer (advertising/consumer counsel, ideally jewelry-literate)

1. **"Man-made diamond" as the house qualifier** — sign off on the formulation in §1.4 (hyphenated, immediate, equal type, plus a "laboratory-grown" spec line on every selling page). Confirm whether JVC would want "laboratory-grown" visibly in H1s too, and whether to adopt "[American Jewelry]-created" once stones are grown in-house.
2. **Implied origin** — does the combination of the trade name "American Jewelry," tier names "Royal American / Americana / New American," and a published "made in America" ambition create an implied Made-in-USA claim that must be qualified on every page? Which exact Tennessee-origin sentence is safe at each stage of the in-house build-out (Part 2 table)?
3. **"Handmade"** — whether any Royal American pieces meet § 23.2; what process documentation to keep per piece; approved substitute phrases.
4. **Warranty drafting** under Magnuson-Moss (Full vs Limited, "lifetime" definition, inspection conditions, tie-in rule, state-variation language) and the split between warranty and "care program."
5. **Custom/made-to-order terms** — enforceability of deposit forfeiture after render approval in Tennessee; whether Tennessee requires a posted refund policy; Mail Order Rule compliance for production timelines.
6. **Privacy** — confirm TIPA and CCPA non-applicability on revenue; required consents for email/SMS; pixel disclosures; breach-notice plan; whether to add an arbitration clause to Terms.
7. **Pre-owned Rolex** — nominative-fair-use boundaries after *Rolex v. BeckerTime* (5th Cir. 2024): what may be said, required non-affiliation disclaimer, handling of watches with aftermarket parts, authenticity warranty language, AML/cash-reporting obligations for high-value trade-ins. Source: https://trademarklawyermagazine.com/rolex-wins-injunction-but-no-damages-against-beckertime-due-to-laches/
8. **Sourcing claims** — "conflict-free," "ethically sourced," non-Russian representations: what supplier paperwork (System of Warranties statements, origin declarations) must be on file before the words appear; whether to say anything beyond "not mined" for man-made stones.
9. **Future diamond growth in Tennessee** — rules for "grown in Tennessee," renewable-energy and water claims (Green Guides §§ 260.5/260.15), and whether a grower name on reports ("[name]-created") changes the required qualifier.
10. **Financing** — review Affirm disclosures and any in-house layaway/financing against TILA/Reg Z and Tennessee law.
11. **ADA** — confirm accessibility target (WCAG 2.2 AA), statement wording, and vendor contract language for the builder/checkout components.
12. **Comparative tier copy** — any sentence comparing natural and man-made on value, rarity, environment or "realness" (NAD precedent cuts both ways).
13. **Appraisals** — permissible language around "retail replacement value" vs purchase price; staff-appraiser independence; no appraisal-as-discount comparisons (16 CFR 233).
14. **Reviews** — solicitation flow, employee/insider rules, and the Google-reviews display and wording under 16 CFR 465.
15. **One-of-one promise** — contractual wording for "never reproduced" on Royal American and remedies if the house ever reissues.
